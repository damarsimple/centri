# Independent review — `paper/centri-ieee.tex`, 2026-08-03

Commissioned critique of the IEEE draft, written against the draft plus the project's own source
documents, the P-MAGIC paper, the live workspaces and the analysis code. Findings marked
**[VERIFIED]** were re-checked independently afterwards; **[RELAYED]** are the reviewer's, not
yet re-checked; **[REVIEWER WRONG]** are corrections to the review itself.

## Verdict

**Weak reject — borderline, recoverable.** The two-judge reliability study is a genuinely
publishable methodological contribution that this literature mostly lacks, and the paper is
unusually honest. Three things sink it as written: the sensor table describes a different pipeline
run from the evaluated corpus; the headline difficulty result is enforced by the pipeline's own
gate rather than observed; and one corroboration sentence is contradicted by the paper's own data.
The fixes are mostly subtraction plus one re-run.

## 1. Factual errors

### E1 — The sensor table is a different run from the corpus. **[VERIFIED — see TODO §1.4]**
Table VI comes from the archived `job_turntable-3-rect`; the corpus comes from
`workspaces/job_turntable-3`. **Identical video (same md5) and identical sidecar**, two runs:

| | archived `-rect` (the gyro table) | shipped (the corpus) |
|---|---|---|
| `r_fit_m` | 0.1477 | 0.2037 (**+38%**) |
| `mean_omega` | 5.794 | 5.458 |
| `mean_ac` | 5.686 | **7.449** |
| `max_ac` | **14.127** | **31.059** (**2.2×**) |
| `period_s` | 1.1024 | 1.4692 |
| rectification | not applied (tilt 5.6°, axis ratio 0.995) | applied (tilt 43.0°, axis ratio 0.732) |
| outliers rejected | 0 | 20 |

The table's "peak $a_c$ 14.13" **is** the archived `max_ac` = 14.127, and its "peak ω 9.781" is
exactly $\sqrt{14.127/0.14768}$.

Three consequences the reviewer draws that go beyond the earlier finding:
1. On the **shipped** run, peak ω is $\sqrt{31.059/0.2037}$ = **12.35** rad/s vs the gyro's 9.820 —
   **+26%**, not 0.4%. Peak $a_c$ is **+119%**, not 0.5%. Even jump-corrected (31.06 → 18.12) it is
   +28%.
2. The draft's caveat is aimed at the wrong problem. The gyro-validated run has **zero rejected
   outliers and 100% coverage** — the tracker jumps belong to the *other* run.
3. **The divergence came from the agentic half, not the frozen half.** `pipeline_inputs.json`
   differs between the runs in `fps` (59.769 vs 59.940), `hub_px`, `reference.diameter_px`
   (1025 vs 1078) and `roi_crop` — all agentic outputs from an identical sidecar. So §III-A's
   *"a gate can only check prose against a measurement if the measurement is the same measurement
   twice"* is true of the frozen half and **materially misleading about the delivered product**.

### E10 — "8.18–8.58/10" is P-MAGIC's *linguistic relevancy* row, not an overall band. **[RELAYED]**

### E12 — The judge's documented **false positive** is omitted. **[RELAYED]**

confidently and arithmetically wrong is a bad look — and it weakens Table X directly.

### E13 — Table X lists as a "defect" something §V-C says is not one. **[RELAYED]**
The reading-grade row contradicts §V-C's *"This is not a defect in the material."* And the
diversity row is a category difference, not a detection result — no other layer is even defined at

## 2. Reviewer attacks, ranked

**A1 — The 7/7 ladder is enforced, not observed.** *(Not answered. Decides the paper.)* Confirmed
independently — see TODO §1.1. *Strongest available answer:* lead with `cognitive_demand`, which is
enforced by nothing, rises for both raters, and is the best-agreed criterion (κ = 0.59, r = 0.78).
Report EI as an invariant the pipeline maintains. A sign test (7/7 monotone, null p = 1/6 per clip,
p = 4×10⁻⁶) is worth running — but only on a measure that isn't enforced.

**A2 — Table VI validates a run that isn't the corpus.** *Strongest answer:* re-run against the
shipped workspace and publish the honest numbers. Means within a few percent, peak ω +26%, peak
$a_c$ +119% is a **better** paper — it is exactly the "averages trustworthy, instants not" thesis,
now actually supported, and it converts §V-F's tracker-jump anecdote into a quantified failure with
ground truth behind it.

**A3 — The paper's strongest evidence is a single-rater absolute score, two pages after proving
those are meaningless.** Axis 4 has one rater, no κ, n = 7 per level. Worse: annotation correctness
is scored **against the seed**, so on `turntable-3` an annotation faithfully drawing a 27%-wrong
measurement scores full marks. *The paper's own "tracing is not validating" lesson applies to its
strongest row and is not applied.* *Strongest answer:* argue the annotation contribution from the
deterministic checks (`verify_figures` clean on 7/7, plus 6 + 7 pinning tests) with the rater as
corroboration.

**A4 — "Each layer caught a defect the others missed" is an anecdote.** Of five rows: two are
metrics only one layer computes (definitional); one is an unchecked assertion — *you have the
rationale text for all 252 ratings, this is a 20-minute check*; one was actually found by a
hand-written sweep, not the judge; one is clean. *Strongest answer:* demote to a **coverage
argument** — each layer is *structurally capable* of seeing a class the others cannot, exhibited in
two load-bearing cases.

**A5 — The judge is the writer's own family, and the second rater doesn't settle it.** The paper
concedes but does not analyse, and cites **zero** LLM-as-a-judge literature. *Strongest answer, and
it is in the data:* self-preference should bite hardest where the writer's failures live — but on
`grounding_accuracy` the writer's family is **not** more generous (3.10 vs 3.14) and on
`difficulty_fit` they are identical (4.48 vs 4.48); the large gaps are on `fluency` (−1.05),
`structure_clarity` (−1.00), `comprehension` (−0.90), i.e. surface rows where harshness is the more
parsimonious account. **But the flip side must also be said:** on the one known-wrong worksheet
rater A gave grounding 5 and rater B gave 2. Honest position: *rater A cannot be trusted on
grounding; every grounding conclusion should come from rater B or the deterministic gate.*

**A6 — n = 7, one topic, one lab, no learning outcomes.** Answered honestly but not enough. The
source carries a caveat the paper drops: *"n = 21 is small for a per-criterion κ… read as
indicative."* The paper's second-biggest claim (grounding κ = −0.04) is one of those indicative
values, quoted in bold four times. With n = 21 the CI spans most of [−0.4, 0.4]; the honest claim is
**"no detectable agreement"**, not "κ = −0.04".

**A7 — One clip with knowingly wrong numbers is reported rather than fixed.** The position is
admirable, but `turntable-3` is load-bearing in four places and the paper never says what the
numbers would be without it. *Strongest answer:* report **n = 21 as scored and n = 18 withdrawn**
side by side. If conclusions are unchanged, one sentence neutralises the attack.

**A8 — The system is less automatic than it reads.** Table IV's last row: the hints file is
*"edited by hand"*, per clip, on a 7-clip corpus — and §III-G calls first-pass steering *the durable
lever*. *Strongest answer:* regenerate one or two clips with the hints file empty and report the
gate delta. Even n = 2 beats silence.

### Is the technical/process detail sufficient? — **No, and it is in the wrong places.**

Good and sufficient: the seed contents, tiers-as-seed-restrictions, Alg. 1, and especially §III-F's

Missing, in order of how badly a reviewer wants it:

5. Gate tolerances unquantified ("within tolerance", "to three significant figures").
6. Cost and latency — a three-number footnote this venue asks for.
7. **`video_recognition_quality`**, named in `eval-framework.md` as one of four claimed novelties,

## 4. Missing

**Baselines — there are none.** Not against a text-only prompt with the numbers pasted in; not
against P-MAGIC's own numbers in the table built to hold them; not against a no-gate ablation; not
against the **generate-K-and-select variant already implemented behind a flag** — a free ablation
sitting in the codebase that directly addresses the paper's stated weakest component.

**Ablations, all cheap:** (a) gate on/off — the whole justification for §III-G; (b) hints file
on/off; (c) **seed-restriction vs "write it harder"** — this is *the* claim of §III-E and it is
asserted with zero evidence. One extra run with an instruction-only tier control converts the
paper's central design claim into a result.

**Statistical treatment.** No CIs anywhere. No significance test on any ladder. No test that the
raters' offset is constant (eyeballed from three numbers). No multiple-comparison awareness across
12 × 2 + 11 criteria. *"For a paper whose thesis is that a single judge run produces a number that
looks like a measurement, the absence of uncertainty on its own numbers is the most quotable irony a
reviewer can find."*

**Reproducibility artifacts.** No code/data statement, no repository, no prompt appendix. The
frozen-prompt method is the paper's most reusable contribution and there is no way to reuse it —
`judge-reliability-2026-07-31.md` §7 already contains a working reproduction script, ready to paste
as an appendix.

**Ethics / IRB.** Absent. P-MAGIC, same institution, carries IRB approval and informed consent. This
paper has no human subjects *yet*, but it plans a teacher panel, filmed a playground roundabout and
a scene with a hand entering frame, and sent rendered frames of that footage to a third-party API.

**The biggest citation gap: zero LLM-as-a-judge literature.** The central methodological claim is
about judge reliability, and the paper cites neither the paradigm, nor self-preference bias
(directly relevant), nor position/verbosity bias, nor judge–human agreement work. *"You are
re-deriving known results without acknowledging them, which also means you cannot say what is new in
your finding."* Also absent: any AQG survey, cognitive-load/worked-example literature beyond one
Sweller, any citation for the known failure of readability formulas, and the SAM 3 release.

**Threats not covered:** the EI circularity; perception-half run-to-run variance (now measured at
38%); **order/anchoring** — rater B saw 3 worksheets per agent, rater A saw 21 in one pass, and the
source explicitly notes B *"could not calibrate its scale across the corpus"*, which is a plausible
partial explanation of the −0.5 offset the paper attributes to calibration; rater B's test–retest
was never measured, so only one side of the comparison is pinned; and the OpenStax reference was
*"reorganised into our section structure"* — by whom, how, and does that inflate BERTScore?

## 5. Writing

Prose is *"well above average for this venue"*; §III-F and §V-D are singled out as genuinely good.

**Self-flagellation crosses into liability in one specific way: the paper retracts claims the reader
never heard.** An IEEE reviewer has not seen the plan deck, the week, or the offline run. Four
passages do this (the 7.8/8.1/11.7 reading-grade retraction; the "checker has been right"
withdrawal; the zero-new-gate-failures retraction; the 21/21-vs-19/21 offline run). **The finding
survives without the retraction in every case** — e.g. *"A deterministic grounding check verifies
that a number traces to the measurement; it cannot ask whether the measurement is sound. Tracing is
not validating"* carries the whole content and is stronger. **Keep exactly one** — the reading-grade
one, because a negative result on your own difficulty claim is the paper's credibility anchor.

**Overclaiming, three phrases** — each the sentence a hostile reviewer will quote back:
*"the strongest empirical claim"* (it is the weakest); *"independently checked… and found them
right"* (a single rater's self-report presented as verification — *"the rater reported checking"* is
honest); *"the single most consequential design decision"* (undercut by E1).

**Hedging that reads as evasion, one place:** §V-A's *"strongest caveat in this paper"* is pointed at
the wrong problem, so once a reviewer finds E1 it looks pre-emptive rather than informative.

**Does it hang together without Abstract and Introduction?** Mostly, but §II opens on a cliff — the
first sentence names Centri with no prior statement of what it is. **What the Introduction must
establish:** concrete grounding before any positioning (the commented draft's opening does this —
keep it); the three claims as claims, with claim 1 rewritten after E1 and claim 2 after A1; the gap
sentence once, in the Introduction rather than buried in §II-C; an **explicit non-claim** early
("neither multimodality nor annotation is novel against the sibling system") — this is what stops a
reviewer spending the whole review on it; and a pointer to the output figure. **What it must not
do:** *"The third point is the one we would most like to be read"* — do not put a preference for how
you would like to be read into an IEEE introduction.

1. **Re-run the gyroscope comparison against the shipped workspace and publish those numbers**
   (half a day; log, workspace and alignment script all exist). Removes the one disqualifying error,
   and the honest result fits the paper's own thesis better than the current one. Add a paragraph

   — *"converts your worst embarrassment into the paper's most original engineering finding."*
2. **Rewrite the two claims your own data contradicts — E2 and A1** (two hours, no experiments).
   The true version of E2 is a *stronger* argument for two raters than the false one.
3. **Cut §III by half and add the missing output figure** (one day). 11 pages → 8, and "no reader
   ever sees the artifact" → "the artifact is the paper's best evidence."
4. **Add the LLM-as-a-judge literature and the self-preference analysis** (half a day). Without it
   the central contribution reads as unaware of its own field. With it: *most judge studies report a

5. **Three cheap credibility items** (half a day): CIs on κ with the source's own "indicative"
   caveat; n = 18 alongside n = 21; an ethics and artifact-availability paragraph.
