\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Centri generates physics learning material from smartphone video of circular motion. Its
kinematic curves are visibly ragged where the parent system's (P-MAGIC) are a clean line.
This report establishes the cause per phenomenon across the full 11-template corpus.

Two findings dominate. First, on one clip (\texttt{roundabout-4046}) \textbf{the ragged
$\omega(t)$ is not noise and not tilt: it is true perspective projection}. The natural remedy
(fit the elliptical image of the orbit, stretch it back to a circle) removes only $12\%$ of it,
because an affine model addresses the wrong harmonic. An uncalibrated projective rectification,
derived from the imaged rotation axle and the orbit conic, removes the artifact while leaving mean
$\omega$ invariant. \textbf{This is now implemented in the frozen pipeline} and measured end-to-end:
ripple CV $0.167 \rightarrow 0.031$, phase-locked fraction $0.944 \rightarrow 0.057$, mean
$|\omega|$ moving $0.2\%$, with the other six clips of the golden set bit-identical and their
refusals recorded with reasons.

Crucially, \textbf{this does not generalise, and the reason is the useful result}. Applying the
same rectification to the two other clips that a tilt-based reading nominates
(\texttt{bicycle}, \texttt{turntable-1}) changes nothing, correctly. \textbf{Perspective
strength is the offset between the imaged axle and the centre of the elliptical path --- not
the tilt.} That offset is $85.6$\,px on 4046 and $0.7$--$8.8$\,px on every other clip, an order
of magnitude apart. All of these clips are tilted $15$--$27^\circ$; tilt determines only that
the orbit \emph{images} as an ellipse, not that the projection is perspective rather than
affine. An earlier draft of this report claimed the artifact was corpus-wide; that claim was
produced by a classifier that read the harmonic ratio without checking whether the ripple was
phase-locked at all, and it is retracted in \S\ref{sec:null}.

Second, \textbf{detection fails on $\sim$20\% of the corpus, always by the same mechanism}: the
text prompt matches a background object of the same shape or colour. \texttt{"fan blade"}
tracks window louvres; \texttt{"black knob"} tracks foliage. These clips report
\texttt{track\_coverage: 1.0} while being wrong in over a third of frames.

Third, \textbf{the two clips whose curves looked cleanest were not measurements at all}: their
orbits are synthesized by \texttt{frequency} mode, which carries a floor at $1.0$\,rad/s and so
reports the fan spinning while it is provably at rest ($0.002$\,rad/s). The blade-pass \emph{rate}
is nonetheless correct (verified against the real red marker, correlation $0.974$); measuring the
blade-tip scale with a ruler ($559$\,px/m) and interpolating the FFT bins to remove a quantisation
staircase make $v$, $a_c$ and $\omega(t)$ trustworthy. But because the per-frame orbit is
reconstructed from the rate, these clips are \textbf{silver} --- teachable numbers, reconstructed
trajectory --- not gold.

Fourth, and found only after the first edition of this report, \textbf{4046 carried a second and
larger defect that this report's own audit missed}: the tracker ran on a cropped copy of the video
while the axle was detected on the full frame, and both were declared full-frame, so the pipeline
measured a trajectory against a centre $232$\,px away from it. Radius spread, $\sigma_\omega$ and
peak $a_c$ were inflated by factors of $3$--$10$, and the validation flags that fired blamed the
physics. \textbf{A file whose mean $\omega$ implies a $0.80$\,s lap while reporting $1.072$\,s
should not have needed a second pair of eyes}; the check that catches it is now deterministic, and
a later run reproduced the same mis-declaration on a different clip.

Of four phenomena we can now vouch for, \textbf{only one required correcting the data}; the other
three were recovered by fixing our own diagnostics and configuration. With projection removed, what
limits that clip is no longer the geometric model --- only $1.3\%$ of the residual is
phase-locked --- but the tracked marker itself, a $108\times119$\,px knob whose apparent size varies
with viewing angle: the next gain is a better sticker, not a better model. We report what did
\emph{not} work --- including four of our own claims falsified by frame-by-frame inspection, a
tracker bug we documented and then re-committed, and two end-to-end runs destroyed by a hint that
asked the agent for a fix the architecture forbids --- and locate each fix in the architecture:
geometry is deterministic and belongs in code, while per-clip judgement belongs to the agent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and scope}

The parent system produces $\omega(t)$ curves that read as a single clean line. Centri's, from
comparable phenomena, oscillate. Because the material generator narrates these curves to
students, the distinction is pedagogical, not cosmetic: on \texttt{roundabout-4046} the
uncorrected curve swings between $5.5$ and $12$\,rad/s roughly $18$ times in $15$ seconds,
which tells a student the wheel repeatedly speeds up and slows down. It does not; it coasts
smoothly from $9.4$ to $6.5$\,rad/s.

This report answers, per phenomenon: \emph{what is actually wrong, what fixes it, and what
cannot be fixed downstream at all.}

\subsection{A note on method: statistics did not survive contact with the frames}
\label{sec:method-note}

Two claims in the first draft of this investigation were quantitative, plausible, and wrong.
Both were produced by computing a statistic over a track nobody had looked at:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{``\texttt{roundabout-4048} is filmed at a $29^\circ$ tilt.''} This was a conic
  fitted to a point cloud consisting substantially of trees and shadows. The number described
  the tracker's failures, not the scene.
  \item \emph{``The orbit is an ellipse, so squash it back.''} Correct about the ellipse,
  wrong about the remedy (\S\ref{sec:affine}).
\end{enumerate}

Consequently this report enforces an order that the rest of the pipeline should adopt:
\textbf{detection is verified first, by overlaying the cached track on real frames; geometry is
computed only afterwards, and only for clips that pass.} A geometry number from an unverified
track is not a measurement. The same discipline caught two further errors in our own survey
tool (\S\ref{sec:survey-bugs}).

\section{Diagnostic method}

\subsection{Detection gate}
\label{sec:detection-gate}

For each clip, from the cached trajectory: coverage, the per-frame step distribution, and the
coefficient of variation of the detected bounding-box area. A rigid object's apparent size is
near-constant, so a wildly varying box area means the detector is switching targets.

\textbf{The step bound must be angular, not a fixed pixel count.} Our first gate flagged any
step over 100\,px as a teleport. That is wrong, and it produced a false accusation against
\texttt{computerfan-4029} (\S\ref{sec:4029}): at $r = 312$\,px and 4\,rev/s a \emph{correctly}
tracked marker moves 131\,px per frame. The meaningful quantity is the angle swept per frame:
below ${\sim}90^\circ$ the point is unambiguous, above $180^\circ$ the spin is aliased and
unrecoverable at that frame rate. 4029 peaks at $55^\circ$/frame. A pixel threshold cannot
distinguish a fast object from a teleporting one; only comparison against the physical
expectation ($\omega r/\mathrm{fps}$) can.

\textbf{Amended 2026-08-06: angular alone is not sufficient either.} The paragraph above is
right that a fixed pixel bound is wrong, and wrong to conclude that an angular bound is
therefore right. An angular gate is blind to \emph{radial} displacement by construction, and
\texttt{turntable-3}'s three $604$--$713$\,px teleports are largely radial: this gate scored that
clip $\mathtt{Jmp} = 0$ while the marker was flipping between two positions $728$\,px apart, and
$27\%$ of its taught $a_c$ was the consequence (\S\ref{sec:tt3-jumps}). \textbf{The gate needs
both bounds --- angular \emph{and} radial --- each relative to the clip's own local median rather
than to a constant.} A rigid marker on a rigid rig should also hold its radius: a radius CV above
${\sim}0.1$, or any frame beyond twice the median radius, convicts on its own and needs no
threshold tuning at all.

\subsection{The harmonic fingerprint}
\label{sec:fingerprint}

If $\omega(t)$ ripples, the cause is identified before anything is applied. We detrend $\omega$
against time (cubic), then regress the residual on harmonics of \emph{orbital phase}. The ratio
of the 1-per-revolution amplitude $A_1$ to the 2-per-revolution amplitude $A_2$ names the cause.
Each candidate has a distinct signature, established by simulating a known-uniform spin under
each distortion (Figure~\ref{fig:fingerprint}, left):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
$A_1/A_2$ & Cause & Remedy \\
\midrule
$\approx 0$ (pure 2/rev) & affine tilt (foreshortening only) & ellipse $\to$ circle axis stretch \\
$\approx 1$--$3$ (mixed) & \textbf{true perspective} & projective rectification (\S\ref{sec:method-rect}) \\
$\gg 10$ (pure 1/rev) & wrong rotation centre & re-fit the centre \\
no phase-lock, broadband & detector/centroid noise & improve detection \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The decisive property: \textbf{a phase-locked ripple is deterministic}. It repeats identically
every revolution, so no low-pass filter removes it without also destroying the real $\omega(t)$
trend it sits on. This is the most expensive available mistake at this stage, and
\S\ref{sec:filter} shows the pipeline currently makes a version of it.

Two guards matter. The classification requires \textbf{at least 2 revolutions} of phase
coverage --- below that, $A_1$ and $A_2$ are not separable and the clip is reported
\und{} rather than clean. And all quantities are computed over the \textbf{active window}
only; a clip that idles then moves otherwise divides its ripple by a near-zero mean $\omega$
and reports a fictitious artifact.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig-fingerprint.png}
\caption{The harmonic fingerprint. \textbf{Left:} a simulated uniform spin under each candidate
distortion. Affine tilt produces almost pure 2/rev; a wrong centre produces almost pure 1/rev;
true perspective produces a mixture. \textbf{Right:} the real corpus. All three diagnosable
clips show the mixed signature of perspective. Amplitudes are read from the survey output, not
hand-entered.}
\label{fig:fingerprint}
\end{figure}

\subsection{How the numbers are computed}
\label{sec:howcomputed}

Every quantity reported per phenomenon is one of a small, fixed set. They are defined here once;
the per-clip sections then only state values. All are computed by the frozen \texttt{analysis/}
pipeline from a single input file (\texttt{pipeline\_inputs.json}); none is re-derived by the
agent (\S\ref{sec:filter}).

\paragraph{From pixels to metres.} The tracker returns a per-frame centroid $(x_t, y_t)$ in
pixels and a per-frame bounding box. One reference object of known real size sets the scale:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{px\_per\_m} \;=\; \frac{d_{\mathrm{px}}}{s_{\mathrm{m}}},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{m}} \;=\; \frac{r_{\mathrm{px}}}{\mathrm{px\_per\_m}},
\label{eq:calib}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\mathrm{px}}$ is the reference's median imaged diameter and $s_{\mathrm{m}}$ its real
size (the sidecar's \texttt{physical\_size}). \textbf{Only $v$ and $a_c$ depend
on this scale}; every angular quantity below is scale-free, which is why a wrong or missing
\texttt{physical\_size} leaves $\omega$, $T$ and $f$ untouched.

\paragraph{Angle, angular velocity, acceleration.} With the rotation centre $(c_x, c_y)$, the
orbital phase of the tracked point is
\begin{equation}
\theta_t \;=\; \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(y_t - c_y,\; x_t - c_x\right),
\end{equation}
unwrapped to a continuous $\theta(t)$. Angular velocity and acceleration are its first and second
time-derivatives, taken with a Savitzky--Golay filter (a sliding low-order polynomial fit) rather
than raw finite differences, so differentiation does not amplify pixel noise:
\begin{equation}
\omega(t) \;=\; \frac{d\theta}{dt}, \qquad \alpha(t) \;=\; \frac{d\omega}{dt}.
\end{equation}
Linear speed and centripetal (radial) acceleration of a point at radius $r$ follow from
rigid-body rotation:
\begin{equation}
v \;=\; \omega\, r, \qquad a_c \;=\; \omega^{2} r \;=\; \frac{v^{2}}{r}.
\label{eq:vac}
\end{equation}
Because $\omega$ is a property of the \emph{whole} rigid body --- identical at every radius --- once
$\omega$ is known, $v$ and $a_c$ may be evaluated at any chosen radius on the body. This is exact,
not an extrapolation: the fans report $v$ and $a_c$ at the blade tip using a ruler-measured tip
radius (\S\ref{sec:fans}). Figure~\ref{fig:onframe} shows every one of these quantities on a real
frame.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig-formula-on-frame.png}
\caption{The equations of \S\ref{sec:howcomputed} on a real frame. The centre $c$ and a tracked
point $p$ define the legs $\Delta x, \Delta y$ of a right triangle, whose inverse tangent is the
orbital angle $\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\Delta y,\Delta x)$; differentiating $\theta$ gives
$\omega$, and at radius $r$ the point has tangential speed $v=\omega r$ (red) and centripetal
acceleration $a_c=\omega^2 r$ toward the centre (purple). This trigonometric chain is what the
point-tracked clips (\texttt{roundabout-4046}, the turntables, \texttt{computerfan-4029}) use
directly. The frame here is a fan: for it $\omega$ instead comes from a windowed FFT of the
\textbf{blade-pass probe ring} (pink), but the \emph{same} radius geometry gives $v$ and $a_c$ at the
ruler-measured \textbf{blade-tip orbit} ($r_{\mathrm{tip}}=0.635$\,m, white).}
\label{fig:onframe}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Period and frequency.} Over a stable-speed window,
\begin{equation}
T \;=\; \frac{2\pi}{\langle|\omega|\rangle}, \qquad f \;=\; \frac{1}{T},
\end{equation}
cross-checked against the dominant peak of the FFT of the phase (or of the probe signal below).

\paragraph{Frequency mode (bladed rotors).} Where no per-frame point survives --- identical,
motion-blurred blades --- $\omega$ is recovered \emph{without} tracking a point. Image intensity is
sampled at a ring of probes around the hub; each blade sweeping past a probe dips that signal. The
dominant frequency $f_b$ of the probe signal is the \emph{blade-pass} frequency, and with $N$
blades
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{rot}} \;=\; \frac{f_b}{N}, \qquad \omega \;=\; 2\pi f_{\mathrm{rot}}.
\label{eq:bladepass}
\end{equation}
$f_b$ is read from a \emph{windowed} FFT (window $T_{\mathrm{win}}$, hop $h$), giving a
time-resolved $\omega(t)$ across spin-up and coast-down. An FFT resolves frequency only to
$\Delta f = 1/T_{\mathrm{win}}$, so taking the raw peak bin quantises $\omega$ into steps of
\begin{equation}
\Delta\omega \;=\; \frac{2\pi\,\Delta f}{N} \;=\; \frac{2\pi}{N\,T_{\mathrm{win}}},
\label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
a non-physical staircase (for the fans, $N=5$, $T_{\mathrm{win}}=2.5$\,s $\Rightarrow
\Delta\omega \approx 0.5$\,rad/s). A three-point parabolic fit about the peak bin recovers the
sub-bin location and restores a smooth, physical $\omega(t)$ (\S\ref{sec:fans}).

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} An ellipse (conic) is least-squares fitted to the orbit; its semi-axes
$A \ge B$ give the \textbf{axis ratio} $A/B$ and an implied \textbf{tilt} $\arccos(B/A)$. The
\textbf{hub offset} is the distance between the imaged rotation axle and the centre of that ellipse
--- the quantity that actually measures perspective (\S\ref{sec:trap}), as distinct from tilt. The
\textbf{harmonic fingerprint} detrends $\omega$ and regresses the residual on harmonics of orbital
phase $\varphi$; the amplitudes $A_k$ of the $\cos(k\varphi)$ terms give the ratio $A_1/A_2$
(\S\ref{sec:fingerprint}). The \textbf{phase-lock fraction} is the share of ripple variance
explained by those phase harmonics ($1$ = perfectly orbit-locked, $0$ = broadband noise), and the
\textbf{ripple CV} is $\operatorname{std}(\omega)/\langle|\omega|\rangle$ over the active window.

\paragraph{What each clip hands the agent.} Two per-clip inputs steer the otherwise-generic
pipeline, and both are stated in each subsection below. The \textbf{sidecar} is a small JSON:
\texttt{tracking\_mode} (\texttt{object} = SAM3 text detection, \texttt{color} = HSV blob,
\texttt{frequency} = blade-pass), the detection \textbf{cue} noun(s), the \textbf{reference} object
and its \texttt{physical\_size}, the marked \texttt{rotation\_center\_frac}, and any
\texttt{tracking\_config} (HSV bounds, ROI, step limits). The injected \textbf{\texttt{hints.md}}
is prose substituted verbatim into the orchestrator prompt (\S\ref{sec:survey-bugs}). A wrong cue
noun or a missing \texttt{physical\_size} is the difference between a golden clip and a fabricated
one, so both are documented as the \emph{context handed to the agent}.

\subsection{Corpus}

Twelve templates, eleven distinct videos, approximately six distinct phenomena. \textbf{Five
clips carry material as of rev.\ 2026-08-06} --- \texttt{roundabout-4046},
\texttt{computerfan-4029}, \texttt{turntable-1}, \texttt{turntable-2} and the re-shot
\texttt{fan-4656}; the rest are parked, superseded or withdrawn (Table~\ref{tab:corpus}). Two
duplications matter for any claim of generality:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{base-template-flick} and \texttt{basetemplate-turntable-3} are the
  \textbf{same clip}. Their cached tracks are byte-identical (350/350 points, both objects) and
  their sidecars share \texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} $[0.5028,0.4573]$. \texttt{flick} is
  \texttt{turntable-3}'s video re-skinned with a narrative \texttt{scene\_context}. The data
  genuinely shows a flick, so the re-skin is honest --- but the reference golden run
  (\texttt{9ed918d0}) is turntable-3's data.
  \item \texttt{turntable-1/2/3} are three clips of one phenomenon (a phone on a rotating base).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Hazard.} \texttt{flick} ships a cached track but \emph{no video}. Because a cache hit
short-circuits tracking entirely, any video uploaded against that template silently reports
turntable-3's trajectory.

\subsection{roundabout-4046 --- the validated case}

\textbf{Scene.} A yellow three-spoke wheel on a red arm with one black knob on the rim, hand-spun and
coasting down; filmed handheld, looking down at the wheel from above and in front.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} Sidecar: \texttt{tracking\_mode: object} (SAM3), cue
\texttt{"black ball"}, reference \texttt{"black ball"} (\texttt{physical\_size: 0.06}\,m),
\texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} $[0.490,0.474]$. \texttt{hints.md}: a yellow 3-spoke wheel with one
black knob on the rim, hand-spun and coasting, \emph{filmed looking down from above and in front}
--- the oblique geometry that produces the perspective artifact below.

\textbf{How it is calculated.} SAM3 returns the knob's pixel centroid $(x_t,y_t)$ in each frame; the
\emph{movement} is read by \textbf{trigonometry}. Relative to the marked centre $(c_x,c_y)$, the
inverse tangent turns a 2-D pixel position into an orbital angle --- ``how far round the circle the
knob is'':
\[
  \theta_t \;=\; \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(y_t - c_y,\; x_t - c_x\right).
\]
Unwrapping across the $2\pi$ seam and differentiating with a Savitzky--Golay filter gives $\omega(t)$
(Eqs.~2--3). A least-squares \textbf{conic (ellipse) fit} to the cloud of $(x_t,y_t)$ gives the tilt
from its semi-axes $A \ge B$:
\[
  \mathrm{tilt} \;=\; \arccos\!\left(B/A\right) \;=\; 27.1^\circ, \qquad \text{axis ratio } A/B = 1.124 .
\]
A \textbf{linear regression of the detrended $\omega$ on harmonics of orbital phase} then gives
$A_1{=}1.594$, $A_2{=}0.657$, and the imaged axle --- located by \textbf{HSV colour thresholding} of
the dark-red boss --- lies $85.6$\,px from the ellipse centre. The fix rebuilds $\theta$ in a

\subsubsection{A second defect on this clip, found after this report's first edition}
\label{sec:coordbug}

The analysis above is computed from the cached tracker output directly, and it is sound. The
\emph{pipeline}, however, was reading the same clip through a broken seam, and this report did not
catch it --- an instructive failure, because the report audits 4046 in more detail than any other
clip and still missed it.

The perception steps track on a \textbf{cropped} copy of the video and detect the axle on the
\textbf{full} frame. Both were then declared \texttt{coordinate\_space: "display"}, so
\texttt{contract.load\_inputs} subtracted the crop offset from both together --- correct for a pair
that really is full-frame, and fatal for a pair that is not. The trajectory and the centre ended
up $232$\,px apart. Nothing downstream could see it: the orbit simply read as real-but-eccentric.

Two things make this worth recording. First, \textbf{the validation flags fired and blamed the

was not being done.}

The defect is now caught deterministically: a trajectory and a centre that share a coordinate frame

turns out, its coordinate handling corrected too.

\subsection{bicycle --- phase-locked, but not perspective (unexplained)}

\textbf{Scene.} A phone threaded through the rear-wheel spokes of a bicycle on a stand --- the wheel
\emph{vertical} --- spun by hand and coasting down.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} Sidecar: \texttt{tracking\_mode: object} (SAM3), cue

\textbf{How it is calculated.} The same trigonometric core: SAM3 gives the phone centroid, the

phase-harmonic regression ($A_1{=}0.846$, $A_2{=}0.286$) the ripple's peak phase is compared against
bottom-of-swing (it lands $138^\circ$ away --- wrong for gravity). Periodic occlusion is tested by
\textbf{regressing the phone's visible mask area on orbital phase} ($R^2{=}0.073$). Scale --- absent
from the sidecar --- is estimated by using the phone's \textbf{rotation-invariant oriented bounding
box} (long side $123.9$\,px) as a known-length ruler against the tracked tire (\S\ref{sec:scale}).

\subsubsection*{A scale cross-check that fails --- and why it is not the blocker}
\label{sec:scale}

Scale is \emph{not} why this clip fails. $\omega$, $T$ and $f$ are \textbf{scale-free} (pure
angle over time; \texttt{px\_per\_m} never enters), and bicycle fails on its $\omega$ ripple. A
perfect scale would leave that ripple byte-for-byte identical. Scale reaches only $v = \omega r$
and $a_c = \omega^2 r$.

That said, this clip's sidecar carries \textbf{no \texttt{physical\_size} at all} --- only
\texttt{label: "rear bicycle tire"} and a bbox centre. Per the data contract, absent means
\emph{the agent estimates it}: bicycle's metres are currently guessed.

Using the tracked phone as a known-size reference (its oriented box is rotation-invariant: long
side 123.9\,px, CV 0.021, better than the axis-aligned bbox's 0.034) against the tracked tire
(outer diameter 484\,px):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
assumed phone body length & $\rightarrow$ px/m & $\rightarrow$ derived tire diameter \\
\midrule
140\,mm (the 5.5$''$ \emph{screen diagonal} --- wrong dimension) & 885 & 54.7\,cm \\
\textbf{150\,mm} (typical 5.5$''$ body) & 826 & \textbf{58.6\,cm} \\
158\,mm & 784 & 61.7\,cm \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A real wheel is \textbf{66--70\,cm} outer (26$''$ MTB $\approx$ 66, 700c $\approx$ 68). Inverting:
a standard 66\,cm wheel demands a \textbf{169\,mm} phone body --- a large phone, not a 5.5$''$ one.
\textbf{Two independent references disagree by ${\sim}12\%$, so one is wrong.} The prime suspect is
that the phone is threaded \emph{through} the spokes, so they occlude it and the mask --- hence the
obox --- is systematically short. That is independent evidence the phone's mask is unclean, which
is suggestive for the unexplained ripple without confirming it.

\textbf{Trap:} ``5.5 inch'' is a \emph{screen diagonal}, not a physical edge; the obox long side
measures the phone's \emph{body length}. Confusing them is a ${\sim}7\%$ error straight into $v$
and $a_c$. Settle it with a ruler on the phone body and the wheel.

\textbf{Verdict: OPEN.} A phase-locked ripple that is neither perspective nor occlusion (cause
unknown), on a clip whose scale is also only estimated --- we cannot yet say what its data should be.

\subsection{turntable-2, turntable-3 / flick --- undecidable, not clean}
\label{sec:corpus-tt}

\textbf{Scene.} A red phone on a black circular base, filmed nearly top-down: at rest, one flick
($t\approx2.1$\,s), then a coast to a stop.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} Both carry the turntable sidecar: \texttt{tracking\_mode:
object}, cue \texttt{"red phone"}, reference \texttt{"black circular base"}
(\texttt{physical\_size: 0.4}\,m). The marked \texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} is $[0.515,0.419]$
(t-2) and $[0.503,0.457]$ (t-3\,$=$\,flick). \texttt{flick}'s \texttt{hints.md} flags it as the golden
template (job \texttt{9ed918d0}): rest, one flick at $t{\approx}2.1$\,s, then coast-down.

\textbf{How it is calculated.} Same trigonometric angle (Eq.~2) and Savitzky--Golay
$\omega$. The difference is coverage: the harmonic fingerprint needs $\ge 2$ revolutions to separate
$A_1$ from $A_2$, and these clips give only $1.4$ and $1.8$ --- so the fingerprint is left undefined
and they are reported \und{}. The \textbf{hub offset}, a \emph{static} measurement (imaged spindle vs
ellipse centre, needing no revolutions), still applies: $3.4$ and $0.7$\,px $\Rightarrow$ no
perspective. Near face-on (axis ratio $1.002$) the conic axes are ill-conditioned, so rectification
is deliberately withheld.

\textbf{Found.} Both tracks are clean. Both are \emph{too short to diagnose}: 1.4 and 1.8
revolutions of active motion against a floor of 2. Their tilts are real ($11.3^\circ$ and
$3.8^\circ$) but their $A_1/A_2$ cannot be separated.

This distinction matters and is easy to lose: \textbf{\und{} is not a clean bill of health.}
\texttt{turntable-3}/\texttt{flick} is the least-tilted clip in the corpus at $3.8^\circ$ and
the closest thing we have to a well-captured example --- but ``least tilted'' is not
``untilted'', and its apparent quality was decided by the tripod, not by processing.

\textbf{Improved.} Nothing, and rectification must \textbf{not} be applied: at an axis ratio of
1.002 the conic's axes are ill-conditioned, the vanishing line is numerically unstable, and
rectifying would inject error rather than remove it. Near face-on, leave the geometry alone.

\subsubsection{The tracks are not clean: box quality depends on the viewing angle}
\label{sec:tt3}

``Both tracks are clean'' above was measured as coverage and step continuity, and by those it holds.
It does not survive a closer look. \texttt{turntable-3}'s $v(t)$ shows the turntable
\textbf{speeding up} at $t\approx3.05$--$3.15$\,s, midway through a coast-down --- which a coasting
turntable cannot do. The per-frame angular increments carry it ($5.92 \to \mathbf{6.36} \to 6.04
\to 5.62$\,rad/s), so it is in the measurement, not in the smoothing.

Every mechanism we can test is excluded. Not the marker centroid sliding along a phone that images
$460$\,px long while orbiting at $r=397$\,px, because the phone's own \emph{orientation} angle
carries the same ripple. Not motion blur: its imaged size is constant to $1\%$ across a threefold
speed range. Not perspective: hub offset $0.7$\,px, orbit round to $1.002$. Not a bad centre: the
radial residual is $2.1$\,px rms ($0.52\%$). Not a real torque from a table that is not level: a
fixed geometric error produces a ripple scaling as $\omega$, a fixed torque as $1/\omega$, and the
measured exponent is $0.7$--$1.1$.

What \emph{is} demonstrable is that \textbf{the measured box depends on the object's angle}. The
phone's measured long side varies $432 \to 470$\,px with orbital phase, $44\%$ of it phase-locked
(1/rev amplitude $10.4$\,px); a rigid object's length cannot depend on its angle. A rigidity check
--- the phone co-rotates, so its orientation minus its orbital angle must be constant --- spreads
$3.7^\circ$ peak-to-peak, $0.7^\circ$ rms, $36\%$ phase-locked. A mask that loses an end region at
certain angles shifts the centroid \emph{and} rotates the fitted box together, which is exactly the
coupled behaviour observed; reading the orientation agreement as evidence \emph{against} a mask
problem, as we first did, is backwards. The magnitude is not fully accounted for: a $10$\,px length
wobble implies roughly $5$\,px of centroid shift, against a tangential wander of $15$\,px rms.

The error is almost purely tangential, which is what disguises it: $r(t)$ holds at $394$--$399$\,px
all clip and reads as textbook tracking, while everything wrong hides in $\theta$ --- the quantity
$\omega$ is computed from.

\paragraph{The tracker is not at fault: an ablation against a second detector.}
``The measured box depends on the object's angle'' invites an obvious remedy --- change the
detector. We tested it. Swapping the segmentation-and-propagation tracker for
\textbf{LocateAnything-3B}, a structurally different model (an autoregressive box detector with no
mask, no temporal state and no propagation between frames), \textbf{reproduces the same ripple}. On
\texttt{turntable-3} itself the two locally-detrended $\omega$ residuals correlate at
$r={+}0.81$ and the mean rates agree to $1.7\%$, measured on the $79$ frames inside the active
window where both trackers have a real detection. The two angular tracks sit $0.46^\circ$ rms
apart where the disputed ripple is worth $\approx1.8^\circ$ --- the trackers agree with each other
far more closely than either agrees with a clean coast-down.

\emph{Correcting our own first pass:} we initially quoted $r={+}0.98$ and $0.10\%$ for this
comparison. Those were computed over the whole clip, a third of which is the turntable sitting
still --- an interval where both trackers report zero and agree trivially. Any agreement statistic
spanning a dead segment is flattered by it. The active-window figures above are the honest ones,
and they still support the conclusion, just less emphatically.

This eliminates one hypothesis and sharpens another. The \emph{implementation} is exonerated: no
particular segmentation algorithm is producing the artifact. But both trackers are appearance-based,
so an imaged extent that changes with viewing angle is inherited by both, and the angle dependence
therefore moves from a property of \emph{our tracker} to a property of \emph{the footage} --- glare,
self-occlusion and foreshortening of an extended, specular object seen from a fixed camera. The
practical consequence is a redirection of effort: \textbf{this class of error is fixed by a better
marker, not a better tracker.} A small, matte, radially-symmetric fiducial has no angle-dependent
extent to inherit.

Our first pass ran this ablation with the cue each clip's sidecar already carried ---
\texttt{"red phone"}, \texttt{"black ball"} --- and concluded the second detector was too unreliable
to be usable, losing the phone for $0.70$\,s continuously at peak speed and finding the ball in only
$32\%$ of 4046's frames. That conclusion was substantially an artifact of the \emph{wording}.
Dropping the colour adjective, so that the cue is a bare noun, changes the same model's behaviour on
the same footage:

verdict below --- the time-averaged claim is the one that survives.

\paragraph{One dropped frame, and what it does and does not explain.}
The camera dropped a frame at $t=2.99$\,s. Three independent readings agree: the container's packet
timestamps show a single interval of exactly $2.0\times$ the median; $\text{duration}\times\text{fps}$
spans $351.00$ frame intervals while only $350$ frames exist; and \texttt{avg\_frame\_rate}
($59.769$) disagrees with \texttt{r\_frame\_rate} ($59.940$), which is what a missing frame looks
like in metadata. Frame extraction runs at a constant rate, so \texttt{ffmpeg} fills the hole by
repeating the previous image --- $351$ images from $350$ real frames --- and the repeat is a
perfectly valid picture, so both trackers report it faithfully: the object does not move across it.
LocateAnything returned bit-identical coordinates on the pair, which is what made it visible at all.
With time assumed uniform, one motionless step followed by a normal one reads as the turntable
stopping and then making up the distance.

The pipeline now detects this from timestamps and blanks the repeated sample
(\texttt{dropped\_source\_frames}), so the trajectory carries no position the camera never measured.
Detecting it from the \emph{trajectory} would not work: a frozen position is also exactly what a
stationary object looks like, and every clip here ends at rest.

\textbf{It is not, however, the explanation for the bump.} Exactly one dropped frame exists in the
whole corpus, and correcting it moves the spurious mid-coast rise in the delivered $v(t)$ from
$5.76\%$ to $5.09\%$ --- a $12\%$ reduction. The pipeline's $21$-frame Savitzky--Golay smoothing had
already spread the bad sample thin. We report this because our first measurement of the effect, taken
on raw unsmoothed gradients, suggested it accounted for the whole feature; on the delivered signal it
does not. The correction is worth having as a data-integrity guard, not as a diagnosis.

\textbf{Verdict (superseded 2026-08-06): WITHDRAWN.} The verdict below stood until a jump sweep
found that the tracked point teleports. It is kept for the record, then overturned.

\begin{quote}
\emph{GOLD for time-averages, \und{} per-instant. Cleared of perspective by the static hub offset,
and now cleared of any tracker-specific defect, but carrying a ripple of $\approx 17\%$ of mean
$\omega$ that no mechanism we can test accounts for, over too few revolutions to characterise
(\S\ref{sec:phaselocknull}).}
\end{quote}

\subsubsection{turntable-3 withdrawn: three teleports this report's own gate could not see}
\label{sec:tt3-jumps}

The tracked marker's median step is $\mathbf{1.44}$\,px. Three times it moves
$\mathbf{604}$, $\mathbf{614}$ and $\mathbf{713}$\,px in a single frame, at $t = 0.85$, $1.74$
and $2.07$\,s, flipping between two positions $728$\,px apart. \textbf{One of those lands inside
the active window}, so $18$ of $106$ active samples are contaminated. The $21$-frame smoothing
spreads each step into a symmetric ramp ($\approx\pm12$ frames), which is why they render as
mountains rather than steps --- and why $\omega$ climbs $0 \to 10.2$\,rad/s over an interval in
which the tracked position is frozen to $0.1$\,px.

\textbf{Effect on the delivered numbers: mean $a_c$ $7.45 \to 5.85$, max $31.06 \to 18.12$.} The
worksheets taught $7.45$, which is $\mathbf{27\%}$ above the jump-free value. The clip is
withdrawn from the corpus rather than repaired.

\paragraph{Why the gate in \S\ref{sec:detection-gate} scored this clip \texttt{Jmp = 0}.}
This is the more useful finding, because it is about the instrument rather than the clip.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The jumps are radial, not tangential.} The marker's orbit radius is $296$\,px
  median; across these three frames it goes $244 \to 687$, $581 \to 241$, $241 \to 576$\,px. The
  gate measures \emph{angle swept per frame}. A displacement along the radius sweeps almost no
  angle, so an angular gate is blind to it \emph{by construction}.
  \item \textbf{And they land just under the threshold anyway.} The three frames sweep $86.0$,
  $86.1$ and $85.0^\circ$ --- against a gate that fires above $90^\circ$. Even the tangential
  component missed, by four degrees.
  \item \textbf{The gate this replaced would have caught all three.} The original fixed
  $100$\,px rule flags exactly these three frames and nothing else on this clip. It was replaced
  (\S\ref{sec:detection-gate}) because it falsely accused \texttt{computerfan-4029}, where a
  correctly tracked marker moves $131$\,px per frame.
\end{itemize}

So the corpus was swept with a gate that could not see this failure mode, and the previous gate
could not see the other one. \textbf{Neither bound is sufficient alone: a step is suspicious if
it is large in \emph{angle} or large \emph{radially}, and ``large'' has to be relative to the
clip's own local median, not a fixed pixel count.} A cheap sufficient statistic that would have
caught it independently: this clip's radius has $\mathrm{CV} = 0.386$ and $55$ frames sit beyond
twice the median radius --- on a rigid marker on a rigid turntable, that alone convicts.

\textbf{Nothing in the delivered pipeline rejects a jump.} The sweep that found these was written
by hand. A guard remains unbuilt, and it is the top engineering recommendation of this revision
(\S\ref{sec:recs}).

\paragraph{Two effects that were never separated, and now cannot be.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The sensor validation was run on \texttt{job\_turntable-3-RECT} ($r_{\mathrm{fit}} =
  0.1477$\,m) while the shipped worksheets came from the unrectified workspace ($0.2037$\,m) --- a
  $38\%$ radius gap. Since $a_c \propto r$, rectification and jump-rejection may be
  double-counting one error or may be two independent ${\sim}30\%$ errors.
  \item The mid-coast speed-up ($v: 0.73 \to 1.92$\,m/s during a coast-down) sits \emph{outside}
  every jump-contaminated window and remains unexplained.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The cost of the withdrawal is larger than the clip.} \texttt{turntable-3} was the only
clip with a paired inertial trace (\texttt{IMG\_3075.csv}), so the project's single sensor-based
validation goes with it. The nearest replacement --- \texttt{fan-4656}'s blade-pass cross-check
(\S\ref{sec:fan4656}) --- is independent of both the marker and the tracker, but it is
video-derived, not a sensor. That is a weaker claim and is reported as one.

\subsection{fan-4027, fan-4028 --- the synth fabricates the ends; measurement makes them SILVER}
\label{sec:fans}

\textbf{Scene.} A five-blade ceiling fan with a red-paper marker on one blade tip; two-phase motion
--- spins up to a peak, then coasts to rest.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} Sidecar: \texttt{tracking\_mode: frequency}; cue
\texttt{"fan blade"}; \texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} $[0.5271,0.4444]$ (the hub, $\approx(1012,480)$
px); \texttt{tracking\_config} $\{$\texttt{n\_blades: 5}, \texttt{ring\_radius: 180},
\texttt{orbit\_radius\_px: 355}, \texttt{n\_probes: 12}, \texttt{window\_s: 2.5},
\texttt{hop\_s: 0.5}$\}$; reference \texttt{"blade-tip orbit"} with \texttt{physical\_size: 0.635}
(the ruler-measured tip radius, below). The injected \texttt{hints.md} states plainly that the
orbit is \emph{synthesized} and must never be presented as a measured trajectory --- the standing
caveat that makes these clips SILVER rather than GOLD (\S\ref{sec:golden}).

\textbf{How it is calculated (different in kind).} Unlike every clip above, movement here is
\emph{not} read by trigonometry on a tracked point --- a motion-blurred rotor of five identical blades
exposes no point to apply $\operatorname{atan2}$ to. Instead image intensity is sampled at a ring of
probes around the hub; each blade sweeping past a probe dips that signal, and a \textbf{windowed FFT}
of it gives the blade-pass frequency $f_b$. With $N{=}5$ blades the rotation rate is
\[
  \omega \;=\; 2\pi\,\frac{f_b}{N}
\]
(Eq.~6). The per-frame angle and orbit are then \emph{synthesized} from $\omega$, not measured ---
the root of the SILVER caveat. Scale comes from the calibration ratio (Eq.~1), and the tip speed and
centripetal acceleration (Eq.~4) are evaluated at the ruler-measured blade-tip radius.

\textbf{Found.} These clips ship \texttt{tracking\_mode: frequency}: \texttt{freq\_track} recovers
$\omega$ from blade-pass frequency and then \emph{generates} a circular orbit from it. The
signatures are values no real capture produces --- axis ratio exactly $1.000$, box area CV exactly
$0.00$, centroid jitter $0.06$\,px.

\textbf{The mode was never justified for these clips.} The NOTES give the reason as \emph{``SAM3
aliases 5 identical blades''} --- an \textbf{identity} problem, not a speed one, and a unique
marker solves identity outright. There was never an aliasing risk either: at $\omega \approx
4$\,rad/s the marker moves ${\sim}17$\,px/frame $= \mathbf{8^\circ}$/frame, and blade-pass is
3.2\,Hz against a 30\,Hz Nyquist limit. The NOTES even record that the marker \emph{was}
colour-tracked to obtain the geometry (orbit CV 0.05) --- and the trajectory was synthesized anyway.

\textbf{What the synth gets wrong: it has a floor at 1.0\,rad/s.} Below the minimum detectable
blade-pass frequency it emits $1.0$\,rad/s regardless of the truth.

\subsection{fan-4656 --- the re-shoot: a measured orbit, and a rate validated without a sensor}
\label{sec:fan4656}

\textbf{Scene.} The same five-blade ceiling fan, re-shot with a \textbf{yellow card taped along
one blade}. $1920\times1080$ at $59.94$\,fps, $230$\,s, $13{,}781$ frames --- $2.3\times$ longer
than any clip previously processed, and the only clip in the corpus that spins up, holds a speed,
changes speed setting, coasts, \emph{and} comes to rest inside the recording.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} \texttt{tracking\_mode: color}; cue \texttt{"yellow
marker"}; \texttt{tracking\_config} $\{$\texttt{hsv\_lo} $[10,90,90]$, \texttt{hsv\_hi}
$[35,255,255]$, \texttt{roi\_radius: 330}, \texttt{min\_area: 300}$\}$ --- and deliberately
\textbf{no} \texttt{max\_step} (\S\ref{sec:stickylock}).

\paragraph{Detection.} The marker blob covers $11$--$12$k px against a next-largest in-ROI blob of
$\mathbf{11}$\,px: a separation of roughly $1000{:}1$. Coverage is $\mathbf{99.99\%}$ with
$\mathbf{100\%}$ of detections on the orbit and \textbf{zero} steps above $8\times$ the clip's
median. The marker sweeps at most $34.3^\circ$/frame against the $180^\circ$ Nyquist limit --- a
$5\times$ margin, where the old \texttt{frequency}-mode fans had no resolvable point at all.

Three of the four re-shot takes were rejected on measurement, not taste: one is $2.7$\,s of a
face; one is handheld portrait with wooden ceiling louvres inside the marker's hue band, giving
only $37\%$ of detections on the orbit; one jumps to a wooden window frame $22$ times.

\paragraph{The rate, validated without a sensor.} $\omega$ was measured a second time by an
\textbf{entirely independent route}: the intensity of a single $12\times12$\,px patch at $0.6r$,
Fourier-transformed, dominant peak divided by five blades. This uses \emph{no marker and no
tracker}.

establishes is that the \emph{marker-tracking} leg is not introducing rate error --- a narrower
claim than the withdrawn \texttt{turntable-3} gyroscope comparison made, and the honest one now
available (\S\ref{sec:tt3-jumps}).

\paragraph{Geometry: a single clean tilt.} The orbit fits an ellipse with semi-axes
$260.0/214.2$\,px, axis ratio $\mathbf{0.823}$ --- about $34.5^\circ$ off face-on. Decomposing the
radial residual against orbital phase gives $1/\mathrm{rev} = 4.1$\,px against $2/\mathrm{rev} =
19.3$\,px: almost pure tilt, with negligible decentering. This is the cleanest instance of the
$2/\mathrm{rev}$ signature in the corpus, and the clip on which the false ``affine ellipses need
no un-projection'' claim finally bit (\S\ref{sec:affine-retract}).

\paragraph{Scale: the card is the ruler.} No ruler is legible at ceiling distance, and none was
needed. Three tape measurements --- hub to card inner edge $31$\,cm, card $25\times18.5$\,cm, hub
to blade tip ${\approx}51$\,cm --- initially disagreed with the pixels by up to $38\%$. Two facts
resolved it, both confirmed against the footage:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The card lies along the blade}, its long axis $4.2^\circ$ off radial (median over
  $166$ frames), so its $25$\,cm runs radially: the card covers a \emph{span}, not a point.
  \item \textbf{The card's outer edge is the blade tip.} Predicted $R_c + L/2 = 260.0 + 69.5 =
  329.5$\,px against a blade-tip ellipse semi-major of $319.3$\,px measured independently from
  $800$ tip points --- agreeing to $3.2\%$.
\end{enumerate}

So the card occupies the outer $25$\,cm of the blade: inner edge $31$\,cm, \textbf{centre
${\approx}44$\,cm}, outer edge at the tip. Least-squares over the two directly-measured spans
gives $\mathrm{px\_per\_m} = \mathbf{591}$, and the chain closes: $r_{\mathrm{fit}} = 0.4400$\,m
against a tape reading of $0.44$.

\textbf{A $40\%$ calibration error was caught by that reconciliation.} The sidecar first read the
$31$\,cm as hub-to-card-\emph{centre} when it is hub-to-card-\emph{inner-edge}, giving
$\mathrm{px\_per\_m} = 839$ against the correct $591$ --- every SI length $29\%$ too small, and
$a_c$ with it. The general lesson restates Eq.~\eqref{eq:calib}: \emph{``distance to the marker'' is
ambiguous the moment the marker is larger than a point}, and the two spans that form
$\mathrm{px\_per\_m}$ must describe the same one.

\textbf{Verdict: GOLD for rate; GOLD for scale to ${\pm}5\%$.} $\omega$, $T$, $f$ and every ratio
are scale-free and independently cross-checked to under $1\%$. The SI values rest on
$\mathrm{px\_per\_m}$ bounded at $556$--$615$ by its two anchors. The first clip in this corpus
whose orbit is measured, whose rate is corroborated, and whose scale is derived from more than
one span.

\subsection{computerfan-4029 --- clean; our own gate accused it falsely}
\label{sec:4029}

\textbf{Scene.} A red marker on a PC case-fan blade, spun by hand in short bursts.

\textbf{Context handed to the agent.} Sidecar: \texttt{tracking\_mode: color}, cue
\texttt{"red marker"}, reference \texttt{"red marker"} (\texttt{physical\_size: 0.04}\,m),
\texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} $[0.519,0.473]$, \texttt{tracking\_config} $\{$\texttt{hsv\_lo}

\textbf{How it is calculated.} \texttt{color} mode thresholds the red marker in HSV (the config's

inverse-tangent angle (Eq.~2) and Savitzky--Golay $\omega$. What matters here is the \textbf{gate}

\[

teleports. The equivalent angular step $\omega/\mathrm{fps}$ peaks at $55^\circ$, far below the
$180^\circ$ aliasing limit, and all statistics are restricted to the active burst
($6.7$ revolutions).

\section{Cross-corpus findings}

\subsection{Perspective is \emph{not} systematic --- a retraction, and the rule that replaced it}
\label{sec:null}

An earlier draft of this report claimed that perspective was a corpus-level property, on the
grounds that three unrelated rigs (4046, bicycle, turntable-1) all showed a mixed $A_1/A_2$
fingerprint and a ripple CV in a narrow $0.159$--$0.168$ band. \textbf{That claim was wrong and
is retracted.} It was produced by a classifier that read the harmonic \emph{ratio} without
testing whether the ripple was phase-locked at all. A ratio computed on noise is still a ratio.

Running the rectification on the two additional clips falsified it directly
(Table~\ref{tab:null}).

\textbf{The rule that replaced it: perspective strength is the hub offset, not the tilt.} The
offset between the imaged axle and the centre of the elliptical path is $85.6$\,px on 4046 and
$0.7$--$8.8$\,px on every other clip --- an order-of-magnitude separation, with 4046 alone on
one side. This is not a coincidence of thresholds: the offset \emph{is} the perspective
(\S\ref{sec:trap}), so a near-zero offset means the view is essentially affine, the vanishing
line lies near infinity, the recovered homography is near-identity, and there is nothing to
undo. The rectification did nothing on four clips because \emph{nothing was wrong with them}.

Tilt does not predict this. All five clips are tilted, three of them by $15$--$27^\circ$. Tilt
determines only that the orbit \emph{images} as an ellipse; whether that ellipse is an affine or
a projective image of the circle depends on the camera's distance relative to the orbit --- 4046
was shot close with a wide phone lens, the others flatter.

\subsubsection*{Retraction (2026-08-06): ``an affine ellipse needs no un-projection'' is false}
\label{sec:affine-retract}

The sentence that closed this subsection --- \emph{``An affine ellipse needs no un-projection:
$\theta$ read from a correctly-centred ellipse is already right''} --- \textbf{is wrong, and is
withdrawn.} It conflates two different things: the \emph{hub offset} question (is there
perspective convergence to undo?) and the \emph{foreshortening} question (is the image angle the
true angle?). A near-zero hub offset settles the first and says nothing about the second.

The arithmetic is immediate. A circle spun uniformly and imaged affinely with axis ratio $k$
gives image points $(r\cos\phi,\; kr\sin\phi)$, so
\[
\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(k\sin\phi,\ \cos\phi),
\qquad
\frac{d\theta}{d\phi} = \frac{k}{\cos^{2}\phi + k^{2}\sin^{2}\phi},
\]
which swings between $k$ and $1/k$ \emph{twice per revolution}. The image angular velocity of a
perfectly uniform spin therefore ripples by $\pm\tfrac{1}{2}(1/k - k)$ --- and that ripple is a
$2/\mathrm{rev}$ signature, exactly the one \S\ref{sec:fingerprint} attributes to tilt.

\textbf{Why it went unnoticed, and what that says about the method.} The claim was not tested; it
was \emph{inferred} from the observation that rectification did nothing on four clips. But those
four did nothing because they were nearly round (Table~\ref{tab:affine-ripple}), not because
affine images need no correction. The evidence was consistent with the true rule and with the
false one, and the report chose the false one because it was the tidier story. This is the same
error as \S\ref{sec:method-note}, one level up: \emph{a statistic that cannot distinguish two
hypotheses is not evidence for either.}

\textbf{The correction, in code.} \texttt{rectify()} gated only on hub offset, having measured
\texttt{axis\_ratio} two lines earlier and discarded it, so a centred-but-tilted orbit was
returned untouched. It now takes an \textbf{affine branch}: when the hub offset is below
$20$\,px \emph{and} the axis ratio is below $0.95$, the ellipse is circularised from its own
conic --- no vanishing line is needed, and the map preserves handedness, so the sign of $\omega$
and the reported rotation direction cannot flip. On \texttt{fan-4656} the radial residual falls
$7.29\% \to 3.41\%$. The blast radius is exactly one clip: \texttt{roundabout-4046} (hub offset
$95.7$\,px) stays on the perspective path, \texttt{turntable-2} ($0.992$) is round enough to skip,
\texttt{turntable-1} has no hub mark at all.

\textbf{The rule, restated correctly.} \emph{Hub offset selects the \underline{method}}
(projective if large, affine if small); \emph{axis ratio decides whether there is anything to
\underline{do}}. The original subsection heading stands --- perspective is still not systematic,
and the hub offset still predicts which clip needs un-projection. What was wrong is the inference
that the remaining clips needed nothing at all.

\subsection{A temporal gate that could not let go}
\label{sec:stickylock}

The colour tracker accepts a \texttt{max\_step} bound: once locked, only candidates within that
radius of the previous centroid are eligible, which is what stops a marker jumping to a
same-coloured blade. The bound is right. Its release was not.

\textbf{When no candidate fell inside the bound, the frame was emitted as a miss --- and the lock
was left untouched.} So one step longer than \texttt{max\_step} froze the reference point at a
stale position, and the marker could only be re-acquired when its orbit carried it back within
\texttt{max\_step} of that frozen point: \emph{once per revolution}. A brief occlusion, a blurred
frame that fell under \texttt{min\_area}, or a genuine speed-up all triggered it.

The cost, measured:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Clip & Coverage (sticky) & Coverage (fixed) & Note \\
\midrule
\texttt{fan-4656} (\texttt{max\_step: 90}) & $26.9\%$ & $\mathbf{92.6\%}$ & the config that exposed it \\
\texttt{computerfan-4029} (\texttt{max\_step: 300}) & $86.9\%$ & $\mathbf{99.3\%}$ & shipped this way for weeks \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\texttt{computerfan-4029} is the instructive row: it is a \textbf{corpus clip that had been
scored, reported and taught from} at $86.9\%$ coverage, and nothing flagged it, because a
temporal gate discarding frames looks exactly like an object that is hard to see. The $102$
recovered frames all lie on the orbit, and recovering them moved its mean $a_c$ by
$\mathbf{+21\%}$ ($35.84 \to 43.29$\,m/s$^2$).

\textbf{The fix} is a \texttt{relock\_after} count (default $5$): a lock that has explained
nothing for that many consecutive frames is dropped, and the next frame re-acquires on score
alone. On every frame the old code already had, the new code returns the \emph{identical} point
--- maximum positional difference $0.0000$\,px --- so the change is provably additive. The tracker
now also reports \texttt{n\_relocks}, which is the diagnostic that was missing: a healthy clip
relocks zero times, and a large count means the bound is too tight for the motion.

\textbf{Coverage is not correctness, restated.} \S\ref{sec:coverage} makes that point about a
tracker that reports high coverage while following the wrong object. This is its mirror: a
tracker reporting \emph{low} coverage while following the right one, and being believed.

\subsection{A guard that asked the wrong witness: overlays drawn one crop offset low}
\label{sec:cropbounds}

\S\ref{sec:coordbug} recorded a trajectory that had been mis-declared into the wrong coordinate
space, and the guard added to catch it: the contract measures the trajectory against the centre
under each reading of \texttt{coordinate\_space} and keeps the self-consistent one, because a
trajectory and a centre in the same space trace a steady radius. That guard has now failed in the
direction it could not see, on \texttt{computerfan-4029-2}.

\textbf{What shipped.} Every frame overlay for the clip --- including
\texttt{annotated\_image\_basic.png}, which is embedded in all three student worksheets --- placed
the ``centre of rotation'' on a fan \emph{blade}. The orbit circle ran off the fan onto the desk,
and the $r$, $v$ and $a_c$ arrows sat at the bottom of that circle, nowhere near the marker they
label. A student reading the basic tier was shown an inward pull pointing at the table.

\textbf{What did not happen.} No taught number was wrong. The trajectory and the centre were
displaced \emph{together}, so every relative quantity survived intact. Fitting the marker directly
off the cropped video (RANSAC, $650$ inliers, radius CV $0.014$) gives a centre of
$(552.8, 574.4)$ and a radius of $306.9$\,px against the shipped $305.3$\,px --- $0.5\%$. After the
fix, \texttt{mean\_omega} is unchanged to thirteen significant digits. Exactly one number moved:
\texttt{cy\_px}, from $902.4$ to $574.4$, which is \texttt{roi\_crop.y\_off} $=328$ to the pixel.

\textbf{Why the guard chose wrong.} The radius test compares the trajectory against the centre, so
it is only ever as good as the centre it is handed. Here Step 5 supplied a \texttt{bootstrap}
estimate sitting $260$\,px from the hub. Both readings were therefore bad --- radius spread $0.462$
as declared against $0.243$ untouched --- and the test did what it was written to do: it took the
better of two wrong answers, and left the trajectory in display space. A RANSAC refit then moved
the centre onto that trajectory. The result was internally consistent, correct in every physical
quantity, and wrong only against the picture.

\textbf{Why nothing downstream caught it.} \texttt{verify\_figures.py} was built for this exact bug
class --- it recomputes the plotted extent from \texttt{kinematics.csv} and fails a figure drawn in
full-frame coordinates. But \texttt{kinematics.csv} was itself in the wrong space, so the check
compared the error against itself and passed. It verified consistency, not correctness. This is
the tracker-checker lesson (\S\ref{sec:golden}) in a new place: \textbf{a self-consistent frame of
reference cannot be audited from inside itself}, and every check we had lived inside it. The one
witness outside it was the video, and nothing was asking it.

\textbf{The fix} is a precondition that never consults the centre. A trajectory measured on the
cropped video is bounded by that video's own frame by construction --- a detector cannot report a
centroid at a pixel the file does not contain. So points outside the crop rectangle \emph{prove}
display space, whatever \texttt{coordinate\_space} claims and wherever the centre sits. On this
clip the trajectory reaches $y = 1384$ in a crop $1244$ tall. The bound is deliberately
asymmetric: it fires only when decisive ($>4$\,px, $\geq 3$ points, so one freak detection cannot
flip it) and is otherwise silent, leaving the radius test in charge for clips whose trajectory
fits inside the crop --- which is most of them, since a small crop offset hides a display-space
orbit completely (turntable-1, turntable-2). It is a proof where it applies and an abstention
everywhere else, which is the opposite shape from the statistic it now outranks.

\textbf{Blast radius, measured rather than argued.} At the contract boundary the trajectory and
centre are bit-identical for five of six workspaces and changed for \texttt{4029-2} alone. Re-run
end to end, \texttt{stats.json} and \texttt{kinematics.csv} are byte-identical (unchanged SHA-256)
for \texttt{turntable-1}, \texttt{turntable-2}, \texttt{roundabout-4046} and
\texttt{computerfan-4029}. The rule does fire on the latter two --- $15$ points $16$\,px outside on
4046, $8$ points $147$\,px outside on 4029 --- but both were already being cropped, so it confirms
a decision rather than changing one. That is the useful reading: the clips that were right were
right \emph{for the reason the new rule states}, and only the one that was wrong moves.

\textbf{What this says about the crop.} The ROI crop is fitted to contain about $99.3\%$ of the
trajectory, not all of it. The small tail it deliberately leaves outside is what makes the rule
work at all --- the defect was detectable only because the crop is not generous. A future change
that padded the crop until it always contained the whole orbit would silently disarm this check.

\subsection{How much phase-lock does pure noise produce? A null distribution}
\label{sec:phaselocknull}

The footnote in Table~\ref{tab:null} --- ``below 2 revolutions the statistic is not meaningful''
--- was an argument from first principles, and the threshold it defends ($0.60$, the value at
which the pipeline distrusts per-instant $\omega$) was chosen without ever measuring what the
statistic does when there is nothing to find. That is the same mistake as the retraction above,
one level up: a number was trusted because it was computed, not because its null was known.

So we measured it. For each clip we keep the detrended $\omega$ residual exactly as it is ---
same amplitude, same autocorrelation, same everything --- and destroy only its relationship to
orbital angle, by circularly shifting it against the phase. Repeating that a few thousand times
gives the distribution of phase-lock values obtainable from a signal that, by construction, is
\emph{not} phase-locked.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrl@{}}
\toprule
Clip & Revs & Observed & Null median & Null p95 & Verdict \\
\midrule
\texttt{turntable-1} & $2.61$ & $0.106$ & $0.213$ & $0.427$ & indistinguishable ($p{=}0.81$) \\
\texttt{turntable-2} & $1.47$ & $0.517$ & $0.301$ & $0.608$ & indistinguishable ($p{=}0.12$) \\
\texttt{turntable-3} & $1.79$ & $0.260$ & $0.202$ & $0.325$ & indistinguishable ($p{=}0.23$) \\
\texttt{roundabout-4046}$^*$ & $18.6$ & $0.058$ & $0.069$ & $0.297$ & indistinguishable ($p{=}0.55$) \\
\texttt{computerfan-4029} & $11.8$ & $0.042$ & $0.017$ & $0.122$ & indistinguishable ($p{=}0.21$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent$^*$post-rectification, i.e.\ after its real artifact was removed --- correctly reading
as nothing left to find.

Two things follow. First, \textbf{on a short clip the statistic manufactures phase-lock from
nothing}: fitting four harmonics to an autocorrelated residual over $1.5$ revolutions explains
$20$--$30\%$ of the variance on average, and up to $0.61$ at the $95$th percentile on
\texttt{turntable-2}. The shipped $0.60$ threshold therefore carries a $5.2\%$ false-positive rate
on the shortest clip in the corpus. Second, and reassuringly, \textbf{no clip's per-instant ripple
is demonstrably locked to orbital angle} --- so the rule has never actually fired on noise here,
and the clips that need holding back are held back by the revolution count instead, which is the
guard doing real work.

This does not overturn 4046. Its pre-rectification lock of $0.94$ over $18.6$ revolutions sits far
above that clip's null p95 of $0.30$, which is exactly why the effect was real and removable. The
statistic is sound where revolutions are plentiful and an effect is large; it is uninformative
where they are not. \textbf{The correct fix is to stop comparing against a fixed constant and
compare each clip against its own null} --- the surrogate test above costs milliseconds and
removes the arbitrary threshold entirely. That is recommended, not yet implemented.

One claim of ours does survive the same test. The tracked phone's oriented box varies
$432 \to 470$\,px with orbital angle, and that variation \emph{is} locked to phase beyond chance
($p = 0.0497$) --- marginally, on the same short clip, so it should be re-measured on a longer one.
The imaged extent of an extended object really does depend on the angle it is viewed from. What
does not follow, and what we previously implied, is that this produces a detectable phase-locked
signature in $\omega$: it does not.

\subsection{What the corpus does support}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One clip} (\texttt{roundabout-4046}) carries a large, genuinely phase-locked
  ($0.94$ over 18.6 revolutions) perspective artifact, and it is removable.
  \item \textbf{The fans' $0.0^\circ$ entries are not evidence of good capture} --- their orbits
  are synthesized. The two clips that look cleanest in the corpus are the two that are not
  measurements.
  \item \textbf{An unexplained result:} \texttt{bicycle}'s ripple \emph{is} phase-locked
  ($0.72$ over 6.4 revolutions) but is not perspective (offset $4.9$\,px) and is not periodic
  occlusion (visible area versus orbital phase gives $R^2 = 0.073$). Rectification makes it
  marginally worse. The cause is unknown. It tracks an extended object (a whole phone threaded
  through the spokes), so a centroid effect is a hypothesis, not a finding.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Phase-lock proves orbit-synchrony, not projection.} Any effect locked to orbital phase
--- occlusion, an extended object's centroid, periodic lighting --- produces the same signature.
The fingerprint of \S\ref{sec:fingerprint} narrows the candidates; only the hub offset, measured
independently of the kinematics, distinguishes perspective among them. A diagnostic that skips
that check will misattribute noise to geometry, which is exactly what happened here.

not hold on this clip. One clip is not a general claim, but the assumption deserves a test.

\subsection{Detection fails by prompt--shape collision}
\label{sec:prompts}

Three of ten real tracks are unusable, and every failure has the same mechanism: \textbf{the
prompt noun names a shape or colour that the background also contains.}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Clip & Prompt & Actually tracked \\
\midrule
fan-doll         & \texttt{"fan blade"}   & the window's louvre slats \\
roundabout-4048  & \texttt{"black knob"}  & dark foliage \\

\bottomrule

Existing lab guidance holds that simple category nouns track better than elaborate
descriptions. This corpus adds a sharper, partly opposing rule: \textbf{a noun that describes a
shape will find that shape in the background.} \texttt{"fan blade"} is an excellent description
of a window louvre. The failure is not that the prompt is too complex --- it is that the prompt
is not \emph{discriminative within the frame}. A prompt must be chosen against the background,
not against the object alone.

\textbf{The same rule governs the colour backend, with ``feature'' redefined.} SAM3 fails by
semantic/shape collision; colour tracking fails by hue/value collision (\texttt{"black knob"}
against foliage; a cream toy against a cream ceiling). Both reduce to: \emph{the target's
defining feature is shared with the background}. \texttt{fan-doll} fails on \textbf{both}
backends because its toy is semantically hard \emph{and} cream-on-cream --- which is why
switching tracker did not save it, and only a physical marker will.

\subsection{Coverage is not correctness}
\label{sec:coverage}

\texttt{roundabout-4048} reports \texttt{track\_coverage: 1.0} while being wrong in
$\sim$38\% of frames. Coverage measures whether the tracker returned a point, not whether the
point is the object. Every quality gate keyed on coverage will pass this clip. The gate that
catches it is box-area stability combined with a physically-derived step bound.

\section{The golden set: what we can vouch for}

\section{The rectification method}
\label{sec:method-rect}

\subsection{Why the obvious fix fails}
\label{sec:affine}

The natural reading --- ``filmed at an angle, so the circle images as an ellipse; stretch it
back'' --- is a \emph{weak-perspective} (affine) model. It predicts a pure 2/rev ripple, because
foreshortening is symmetric: the orbit crosses the major axis twice per revolution.

\texttt{roundabout-4046} shows a dominant \textbf{1}/rev term (Figure~\ref{fig:fingerprint}),
which affine tilt cannot produce at all. Its origin is true perspective: the near side of the
orbit is magnified relative to the far side, an asymmetry with a once-per-revolution period.
Applying the affine fix there moved the ripple CV from $0.166$ to only $0.146$ --- a 12\%
improvement (Figure~\ref{fig:affinefail}), because it addresses the smaller of the two harmonics
and leaves the larger untouched.

This reasoning applies \emph{only} where perspective is actually present. On the four clips whose
hub offset is near zero (\S\ref{sec:null}) the image \emph{is} an affine view of the circle, the
axis stretch is the correct and sufficient model, and no projective step is warranted.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig-affine-fail-4046.png}
\caption{The affine remedy failing on \texttt{roundabout-4046}. The ripple survives at nearly
full amplitude (the sign flip is a handedness artifact of the eigenbasis, later corrected). The
peaks are spaced one revolution apart --- the signature affine rectification cannot address.}
\label{fig:affinefail}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Uncalibrated projective rectification via the vanishing line}

The enabling geometry: \textbf{for a circle, the polar line of the circle's centre with respect
to the circle is that plane's line at infinity.} Both the conic and the centre survive
projection, so the imaged rotation axle together with the fitted orbit conic yield the vanishing
line directly --- with \textbf{no camera intrinsics, no focal length, no EXIF}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
C = fit_conic(orbit_x, orbit_y)      # image of the orbit (an ellipse)
h = (hub_x, hub_y, 1)                # the IMAGED AXLE -- not the ellipse centre
l = C @ h                            # polar of h = the plane's vanishing line
H = [[1,0,0], [0,1,0], [l0,l1,l2]]   # maps l to infinity -> now an AFFINE image
                                     # then ellipse -> circle by the axis stretch
theta = atan2(...)                   # in the metric-rectified plane
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The trap: the hub is not the centre of the ellipse}
\label{sec:trap}

This is the single way to get the method wrong, and it fails silently. On 4046:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Centre of the elliptical path & $(530.0,\ 912.3)$ \\
The actual red axle boss (detected) & $(532.3,\ 997.9)$ --- \textbf{85.6\,px lower} \\
\texttt{sidecar.rotation\_center\_frac} & $(529.5,\ 909.7)$ --- \emph{2.6\,px from the ellipse centre} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These two points coincide \emph{only} under affine viewing. Their offset \textbf{is} the
perspective, and it is the method's entire input. Pass the ellipse centre as $h$ and the polar
returns the line at infinity, the homography is the identity, and the rectification silently
does nothing.

This has a direct consequence for the sidecar: \textbf{\texttt{rotation\_center\_frac} is
unusable as $h$}. A human asked to ``mark the centre'' taps the middle of the ring they see ---
which is exactly the point carrying no information. The axle must be detected instead. On 4046
this is reliable: the dark-red boss (HSV $\approx$ (0--10 / 170--179, 120--255, 40--160),
roundish, nearest the orbit centre) was found in \textbf{100\% of frames}
(Figure~\ref{fig:hub}). Tracking it per frame also absorbs camera drift for free.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig-hub-4046.png}
\caption{Axle detection on \texttt{roundabout-4046}, 100\% of frames. Green circle: the detected
red hub. Magenta cross: the sidecar's marked ``centre'', which coincides with the centre of the
\emph{elliptical path}. The visible gap between them is the 85.6\,px perspective offset that
drives the rectification.}
\label{fig:hub}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Why this is not circular reasoning}

A sufficiently flexible transform can make any curve look nicer, so ``it got smoother'' is not
evidence. Two independent checks are required and both are reported:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mean $\omega$ must be preserved.} Rectification redistributes angle
  \emph{within} a revolution; it cannot change how many revolutions occurred, since a full
  revolution's average is projection-invariant. Measured on the delivered pipeline:
  $7.819 \rightarrow 7.803$\,rad/s, a shift of $0.2\%$ (prototype: $7.799 \rightarrow 7.800$). A
  moving mean would indicate real signal being fitted away.
  \item \textbf{Circularity must improve without having been optimised.} The vanishing line is
  derived from the detected hub --- never from $\omega$, never from the radial residual. The
  residual falling $5.22\% \rightarrow 3.42\%$ on the delivered run ($5.20\% \rightarrow 1.68\%$
  for the prototype, which additionally removed camera drift) is therefore a free, independent
  confirmation.
\end{itemize}

A rectification derived from the kinematics it then ``improves'' is worthless; any report of
this method should state which inputs came from geometry and which from motion.

Both checks are now \emph{enforced} rather than merely reported: the delivered implementation
rejects a rectification that moves mean $|\omega|$ by more than $2\%$, and records the
before/after radial residual in every run's statistics.

\subsection{What is left after rectification: the marker, not the model}
\label{sec:marker}

With the projective artifact removed, the natural next question is whether a richer geometric
model --- recovering the orbit's full 3-D pose, or abandoning the planar assumption --- would buy
anything further. Measured on the delivered run, it would not.

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{p{7.4cm}rp{4.4cm}}
\toprule
Measured after rectification & & Reading \\
\midrule
radial residual & $16.5$\,px (3.4\%) & the scatter that remains \\
\quad of which locked to orbital phase & \textbf{1.3\%} & a wrong shape model would appear
\emph{here}; nothing left to model \\
apparent size of the tracked knob & $108\times119$\,px & it is a solid object, not a point \\
\quad of that size variation, locked to phase & \textbf{75--82\%} & different faces of it are seen
at different depths as it turns \\
residual scatter relative to marker size & $\approx 15\%$ & the limit on ``where is the handle?'' \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The interpretation is that the surface model is now adequate and the \emph{measurand} is not
well defined: the tracked centroid of an extended, self-occluding body genuinely moves relative to
the body as it rotates. No projective, affine or 3-D model addresses that, because the error is
not in the mapping from world to image --- it is in what ``the position of the object'' means.

For completeness, the 3-D pose \emph{is} recoverable from what we already have, and it corroborates
the planar route: the orbit's vanishing line meets the imaged circle at that plane's circular
points, which must also lie on the image of the absolute conic, giving one equation for the focal
length under square pixels and a centred principal point. On 4046 that yields $f \approx 1116$\,px
and a plane-to-image angle of $27.4^\circ$, against the axis-ratio route's $27.1^\circ$. It buys
nothing for $\omega$ --- planar rectification already recovers in-plane angle up to a similarity,
and $\omega$ is similarity-invariant --- and it is ill-conditioned on near-circular ellipses, so
only 4046 and the park wheel would support it at all. It would matter for \emph{scale}, where a
reference object at a different depth or orientation than the orbit is currently unmodelled.

\textbf{The actionable consequence is a capture instruction, not an algorithm:} a small, flat,
high-contrast marker collapses the blob toward a point. That is the fix that already rescued both
fan clips (a red marker) and is specified for the park-wheel re-shoot (tape on one knob).

\subsection{Independent corroboration}

The pipeline already contains an unrelated mitigation (\S\ref{sec:filter}): a 1-revolution
moving average. A boxcar of exactly one period cancels all harmonics of the orbital frequency,
so its trend is approximately correct by construction --- and it independently recovers
$9.5 \rightarrow 6.5$\,rad/s, against rectification's $9.4 \rightarrow 6.5$. Two unrelated
methods agreeing on the physics is meaningful support that the rectification recovers signal
rather than manufacturing it.

\section{What the pipeline did, and what it does now}
\label{sec:filter}

\subsection{What it did: treat the symptom}

Before rectification was implemented, the system \emph{already detected} this artifact and
responded in two ways, both of which treated the symptom:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A filter.} \texttt{render/figures.py} draws a 1-revolution moving average boldly
  over a faint raw trace. Its own comment reads: \emph{``Oblique-capture clip: the per-instant
  curve is a projection artifact. Show the raw values faintly and the 1-revolution trend boldly
  (the real physics).''}
  \item \textbf{A suppression rule.} \texttt{quality\_signals.py} computes the phase-lock
  fraction and, when the artifact is present, emits \texttt{do\_not\_claim} instructions
  forbidding the material generator from discussing per-instant $\omega$, the shape of the
  $\omega(t)$ curve, or any within-revolution variation.
\end{enumerate}

Both are reasonable given an uncorrected track, and the boxcar's trend is approximately right.
But the comparison is unfavourable on every axis that matters:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{4.2cm}p{5.1cm}p{5.1cm}}
\toprule
 & 1-rev boxcar (former) & Projective rectification \\
\midrule
within-revolution $\omega$ & destroyed; may never be discussed & recovered (ripple CV 0.031) \\
cancellation & imperfect --- fixed window while the wheel decelerates & artifact removed at source \\
what the student sees & a bold line over a faint jagged trace, implying the raggedness is real & one clean line \\
material generation & \texttt{do\_not\_claim} suppression remains & suppression unnecessary \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The system was hiding an artifact it had enough information to remove.

\subsection{What it does now: remove it at source}
\label{sec:shipped}

Rectification is implemented in the frozen layer as \texttt{analysis/rectify.py} and runs inside
\texttt{geometry.calibrate}, \textbf{before any radius or angle is measured} --- which is the only
correct place for it, since a $\theta$ taken from raw image pixels is already wrong. The split is
the one \S\ref{sec:where} argues for, and it survives contact with a real agent:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The agent supplies one observation.} It detects the imaged axle and passes
  \texttt{hub\_px} through \texttt{pipeline\_inputs.json}. It writes no geometry.
  \item \textbf{The frozen pipeline does the arithmetic}, gated on \texttt{hub\_px} being present
  \emph{and} materially offset from the ellipse centre. The threshold is $20$\,px, which separates
  the corpus cleanly: 4046 at $84.6$\,px and the parked park-wheel clip at $60$--$73$\,px are
  rectified; every other clip sits at $0.7$--$8.8$\,px and is left alone.
  \item \textbf{Refusal is recorded, not silent.} Every run carries a
  \texttt{calibration.rectification} block giving the hub offset, the axis ratio, the tilt, the
  vanishing line, the before/after radial residual --- or, when it declined, the reason
  (\texttt{no\_hub\_px}, \texttt{hub\_offset\_below\_20px}). Both fan clips exercise the refusal
  path: the agent supplied a hub and the pipeline correctly declined.
  \item \textbf{Non-rectified clips are bit-identical.} Six of the seven clips in the golden set
  show zero numeric change against their pre-rectification runs, flags included.
\end{itemize}

The guard rails the method demands are enforced rather than merely reported: passing the
\emph{ellipse centre} as $h$ --- the silent failure of \S\ref{sec:trap} --- is refused by the
offset gate rather than quietly performing an identity transform, and a rectification that moved
mean $|\omega|$ by more than 2\% would be rejected outright. Measured on the delivered run, mean
$|\omega|$ moves $0.2\%$.

\textbf{The correction is invisible in the finished material, which is the point.} A worksheet
generated from a rectified clip reads exactly like one generated from a face-on clip; the
difference is that its per-instant $\omega$ is now allowed to be discussed at all.

\subsection{The trust channel: not naming a cause is not clearing it}
\label{sec:trustchannel}

The rule deciding whether per-instant $\omega$ could be narrated required an \textbf{elliptical
image orbit} before it would distrust a phase-locked ripple --- oblique capture being the cause it
had been derived from on \texttt{roundabout-4046}. Any phase-locked ripple on a \emph{round} orbit
was therefore cleared, however large: a synthetic clip with a perfectly circular orbit and $99\%$ of
its detrended-$\omega$ variance locked to orbital phase was reported reliable, with the guidance
``narrate the timeline normally''. \texttt{turntable-3} is round to $1.002$ and went through that
gate carrying the artifact of \S\ref{sec:tt3}.

Motion of the object cannot repeat with orbital \emph{position} rather than with \emph{time}, so
that signature is diagnostic on its own. It now convicts alone, and the axis ratio only decides
whether the guidance may \emph{name} a cause --- on a round orbit it states plainly that the cause is
unidentified rather than asserting a slant that is not there.

A second gap: nothing counted revolutions. Below two, the fingerprint fits four harmonics to about
$1.5$ cycles and can neither convict nor clear --- a caveat this report states (\S\ref{sec:corpus-tt})
and the code never encoded. Clips under that floor with a material ripple are now reported
\texttt{per\_instant\_omega\_unverified}: per-instant claims suppressed, with the honest reason that
this is a limit of the recording rather than a finding about the motion.

The two cases want \textbf{opposite} figure treatments, which is not obvious and cost us a nearly
shipped regression. A diagnosed artifact keeps the 1-revolution boxcar, which cancels the ripple and
preserves the trend --- appropriate on \texttt{roundabout-4046}, where one revolution is $5\%$ of the
record. Applying the same filter to an \emph{unverified} clip is destructive: the window is
$T \times \mathrm{fps}$, which on \texttt{turntable-3} spans $44\%$ of the active record and cut a
genuine $1.44$\,m/s flick peak to $1.09$ --- understating the transient the clip exists to teach in
order to hide a wiggle we cannot even attribute. Those clips now plot the measurement unchanged and
carry a printed note. The bump remains visible because it is the data; what changed is that nothing
presents it as physics.

\section{Where each fix belongs}
\label{sec:where}

An end-to-end test settled this. Per-clip guidance (\texttt{hints.md}) was injected verbatim
into the orchestrator prompt and the agent \emph{correctly ignored} the instruction to rectify,
because the orchestrator's hard rules state: \emph{``Kinematics is FROZEN. Never reimplement,
edit, or `improve' anything under \texttt{analysis/}.''} The freeze exists for good reason --- its
own docstrings record that agent-authored kinematics made \texttt{center\_drift} swing
8.9--1013\,px and \texttt{stable\_mean\_omega} swing 2.9--6.1\,rad/s \emph{on the same video}.

\textbf{This was not a model limitation. It was a misplaced hint.} The knowledge was filed in a
layer with no authority to act on it.

\paragraph{What the misplaced hint actually cost.} The first edition of this report recorded that
the agent refused, and treated the refusal as the end of the story. It was not. A hint that
\emph{asks} for a fix the architecture forbids does not produce a clean refusal every time --- it
produces an expensive one. On two subsequent end-to-end runs the agent accepted the instruction,
began building its own \texttt{rectify.py}, and \textbf{exhausted the 1024\,MB output guard in the
kinematics phase}, shipping nothing and leaving a degenerate contract behind ($x$ spanning
$0.0$--$0.1$\,px, mean $\omega$ of $0.0$). On the one run that did finish, it computed a rectified
trajectory, wrote it to \texttt{rectified\_trajectory.json}, and then --- having nowhere to put it
in the contract --- \textbf{passed the raw trajectory to the pipeline anyway}. The work was done
and discarded.

The fix was to invert the hint: it now states that rectification is frozen, that the agent must not
write one, and that its entire part is to detect the axle and hand over \texttt{hub\_px}. The same
rule was added to the orchestrator's hard rules. The clip completed end-to-end on the next attempt,
having failed twice before. \textbf{A hint that asks a layer to do what it cannot is worse than no
hint at all}, because the model will try.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l p{6.6cm} l}
\toprule
Layer & Owns & Reachable by a hint? \\
\midrule
Steps 1--5 (agentic) & tracking mode, params, prompts, \textbf{hub detection}, refusal & \textbf{yes} \\
\texttt{pipeline\_inputs.json} & the contract seam --- carries per-clip measurements & \textbf{yes} \\
Step 6 \texttt{analysis/} (frozen) & calibration, geometry, kinematics, stats & \textbf{no --- by design} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The split follows directly, and matches the intended small-agent/large-agent architecture:
\textbf{the large agent supplies the method} (once, validated, as code); \textbf{the small agent
supplies the per-clip observation} (detect the axle, emit \texttt{hub\_px} through the contract);
\textbf{the frozen pipeline does the arithmetic} (rectify when \texttt{hub\_px} is present and
materially offset from the ellipse centre). Nothing asks a 35B model to re-derive projective
geometry per job, and the gate leaves near-face-on clips bit-identical.
\textbf{This is now what runs} (\S\ref{sec:shipped}), and the corpus behaved as predicted: one
clip rectified, six untouched and bit-identical, refusals recorded with reasons.

One caveat the split does not remove. The contract seam it relies on is also where the coordinate
defect of \S\ref{sec:coordbug} lived: the agent is trusted to \emph{declare} which pixel space it
wrote, and a wrong declaration is invisible to every layer below. The architecture correctly stops
the agent doing the geometry; it does not stop the agent mislabelling the geometry's input. That
needed a separate, deterministic consistency check, and it fires often enough to matter.

\subsection{A delivery bug worth recording}
\label{sec:survey-bugs}

An earlier attempt instructed the agent to read \texttt{hints.md} itself. It transcribed the
loading code into a generated script, emitted
\texttt{[Step 1] hints.md found (5484 chars) --- AUTHORITATIVE}, and \textbf{never read a word
of it} (zero tool calls). A log line asserting compliance is not compliance: text read into a
file handle at runtime is not text in the model's context. Guidance must be \emph{injected}
into the prompt, not made \emph{available} to the agent --- the pattern
\texttt{material\_tiers.py} already uses for \texttt{material\_hints.md}.

The survey tool itself failed twice in the same family: it initially classified the idle-then-
flick clip as ``perspective'' (dividing the ripple by a near-zero mean $\omega$ over a mostly
stationary clip) and profiled the static reference base as though it orbited. Both are
statistics computed over the wrong window --- the same error as the 4048 tilt, one level up.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One clip carries the finding.} Perspective is validated, and fixed, on
  \texttt{roundabout-4046} alone. Four other clips were tested and show no perspective. The
  corpus supports ``4046 has a strong perspective artifact and the hub offset identifies it'',
  \emph{not} ``Centri's data is distorted by perspective''.
  \item \textbf{The hub-offset rule rests on a 5-clip separation} ($85.6$\,px versus
  $0.7$--$8.8$\,px). The gap is an order of magnitude and the physics behind it is sound, but no
  threshold has been calibrated, and no clip sits in between --- we have not seen an
  intermediate case.
  \item \textbf{Two clips are unexplained, not one.} \texttt{bicycle} carries a phase-locked
  ripple ($0.72$ over $6.4$ revolutions) that is neither perspective nor occlusion;
  \texttt{turntable-3} carries a ripple of $\approx17\%$ of mean $\omega$ that survives every
  mechanism we can test (\S\ref{sec:tt3}). The ablation there narrows the search without closing
  it: a second, structurally different detector reproduces the ripple, so the defect is not in any
  particular tracker --- it is in what the footage presents to \emph{any} appearance-based tracker.
  Note that \texttt{turntable-3}'s ripple is \emph{not} demonstrably locked to orbital angle
  (\S\ref{sec:phaselocknull}); the object's imaged extent is, marginally, but that does not carry
  through to $\omega$. Until these are understood we cannot claim to know what those clips'
  per-instant data should be.
  \item \textbf{A threshold in the shipped gate is uncalibrated.} The phase-lock value at which
  per-instant $\omega$ is distrusted ($0.60$) was never checked against the statistic's null
  distribution, and on the shortest clip in the corpus noise alone reaches it $5.2\%$ of the time
  (\S\ref{sec:phaselocknull}). It has not misfired on this corpus, but it is a constant where a
  per-clip surrogate test belongs.
  \item \textbf{No external ground truth.} Everything rests on internal consistency (invariant
  mean $\omega$; an unoptimised circularity gain) and agreement with the pipeline's independent
  1-revolution boxcar. No independently measured $\omega$ --- a rated fan speed, a metronome, a
  hand-counted revolution --- anchors the corpus. \textbf{This is the most valuable missing
  piece.} The closest substitute we have is the cross-tracker agreement of \S\ref{sec:tt3}
  ($0.02$--$0.13\%$ on mean $\omega$ between two unrelated models), which constrains the
  time-averaged quantities but says nothing about absolute scale, since both trackers consume the
  same calibration.
  \item \textbf{Accuracy is now limited by the marker, not by the geometry} (\S\ref{sec:marker}).
  Only $1.3\%$ of the post-rectification radial residual is phase-locked, so no further shape
  model is indicated; but the tracked knob images $108\times119$\,px with $75$--$82\%$ of its
  apparent-size variation locked to phase, leaving a scatter of $\approx 15\%$ of the marker's own
  size. This bounds what any amount of modelling can achieve on the existing footage.
  \item \textbf{Camera drift is not removed by the delivered rectification.} The prototype tracked
  the axle per frame and reached a $1.68\%$ radial residual; the shipped version uses a single
  static axle and reaches $3.39\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:rect}). The difference is $53\times41$\,px of
  handheld drift. This is a known, unimplemented improvement rather than a limit of the method.
  \item \textbf{The contract seam is trusted, and was wrong once} (\S\ref{sec:coordbug}). The agent
  declares which pixel space it wrote; a mis-declaration is invisible below and cost 4046 a $232$\,px
  offset that the validation flags attributed to the physics. A deterministic consistency check now
  catches it, but the general problem --- an agent-asserted fact the pipeline cannot verify --- is
  unsolved, and other such assertions exist (notably whether a trajectory was measured or
  synthesized, \S\ref{sec:fans}).
  \item \textbf{The corpus is thin and partly duplicated.} Twelve templates reduce to
  $\sim$six phenomena; \texttt{flick} $=$ \texttt{turntable-3}; \texttt{turntable-1/2/3} are one
  phenomenon; \texttt{fan-4027/4028/4656} are one physical fan. \textbf{The five clips carrying
  material as of rev.\ 2026-08-06 are roughly four scenes}, so between-clip variation is not
  independent, and a finding about how much the clips differ from one another is partly a finding
  about how narrow the scene set is.
  \item \textbf{One scale question remains open} and must not be guessed: the phone's body length
  (to settle bicycle's 12\% cross-check). The fan scale is now measured (blade tip $63.5$\,cm
  $\Rightarrow$ $559$\,px/m, \S\ref{sec:fans}), so the fans' $v$/$a_c$ are real; $\omega$/$T$/$f$ are
  scale-free throughout and never depended on it.
  \item \textbf{Two clips are undiagnosable by fingerprint} at 1.4 and 1.8 revolutions, though
  the hub offset --- a static measurement needing no revolutions --- still clears them of
  perspective. Future captures should target $\ge 3$ revolutions of sustained motion.
  \item \textbf{The unused footage adds little breadth.} \texttt{New cases/} holds 11 unused
  clips, but 4039--4044 are six more takes of 4046's wheel, 4047 is 4048's rig, and 4025/4026/4030
  are further fans. The only new phenomenon, \texttt{4031} (an object swung on a string), is
  untrackable: the object is a motion-blur streak on a hand-held pivot, and a \texttt{"pink
  object"} prompt returns 100\% coverage while tracking the \emph{centroid of the smear}. Corpus
  breadth requires new filming, not more processing.
  \item \textbf{The prompt-collision rule is now partly confirmed, on a second model.} It was
  drawn from three failures and never confirmed by re-prompting; the one attempt
  (\texttt{fan-doll}) failed for an unrelated reason. The cue sweep of \S\ref{sec:tt3} supplies
  that confirmation for a bare noun versus a colour-qualified one, across eight clip--cue pairs on
  an unrelated detector. It remains unconfirmed for the shape-collision half of the rule, and no
  cue has been swept on the incumbent tracker itself.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:recs}

\emph{Item 2 of the first edition --- implement rectification in the frozen layer, gated on the hub
offset --- is done (\S\ref{sec:shipped}); 4046's golden numbers moved as predicted
($r_{\mathrm{fit}}$ $0.254 \rightarrow 0.260$\,m). Rev.\ 2026-08-06 completes it: the gate now
also fires on \emph{foreshortening}, which it had been measuring and discarding
(\S\ref{sec:affine-retract}). What follows is what remains.}

\textbf{Added rev.\ 2026-08-06, in priority order.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{A.}] \textbf{Build the jump guard, with two bounds.} Nothing in the delivered
  pipeline rejects a marker that moves $500\times$ its usual step; the sweep that found
  \texttt{turntable-3}'s teleports was written by hand. The guard needs an \emph{angular} bound
  \emph{and} a \emph{radial} one, each relative to the clip's own local median rather than to a
  constant --- neither alone would have caught this corpus's two known cases
  (\S\ref{sec:tt3-jumps}). A radius CV above ${\sim}0.1$ on a rigid marker convicts without any
  threshold tuning, and is the cheapest thing to compute.
  \item[\textbf{B.}] \textbf{State the calibrated span in the sidecar, not in prose.} The span
  paired with \texttt{physical\_size} is sometimes not any object's bounding box --- an orbit
  radius, a tape mark --- and the pipeline then has nothing correct to measure. A
  \texttt{reference\_geometry.diameter\_px} field is now honoured verbatim when present. Use it
  whenever the reference is not a detectable object: on \texttt{fan-4656} two runs from a
  byte-identical sidecar chose radii differing by $6\%$ before this existed
  (\S\ref{sec:fan4656}).
  \item[\textbf{C.}] \textbf{One clip logging its own gyroscope.} Withdrawing \texttt{turntable-3}
  removed the corpus's only sensor-validated clip. \texttt{fan-4656}'s blade-pass cross-check
  (\S\ref{sec:fan4656}) is independent of the marker and the tracker, but both legs are video. A
  single re-shoot with a phone logging on the rotating object restores an external reference, and
  \S\ref{sec:tt3-jumps} shows what that reference can see that nothing else can.
  \item[\textbf{D.}] \textbf{Report \texttt{n\_relocks} in every run summary.} A temporal gate
  that silently discards frames looks exactly like a hard-to-see object
  (\S\ref{sec:stickylock}); \texttt{computerfan-4029} shipped at $86.9\%$ coverage for weeks on
  that misreading.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Capture beats correction.} Every artifact here was cheaper to prevent than to
  fix. Film closer to face-on; ensure $\ge 3$ revolutions; choose a marker that contrasts with
  the \emph{background}, not merely with the object --- and make it \textbf{small and flat}, since
  marker extent is now the binding constraint (\S\ref{sec:marker}), not projection. The ablation of
  \S\ref{sec:tt3} makes this the \emph{only} available fix for angle-dependent extent: swapping in
  an unrelated detector reproduced the artifact at $r={+}0.99$, because an appearance-based tracker
  of any design inherits what the footage presents. \textbf{A better marker is a real remedy; a
  better tracker is not.}
  \item \textbf{Treat the detection cue as a tuned parameter, not a label.} It is the
  highest-leverage variable measured in this report: rewording \texttt{"black ball"} to
  \texttt{"ball"} raised 4046's detection rate from $32\%$ to $85\%$ on identical footage
  (\S\ref{sec:tt3}), and every clip improved. Prefer a \textbf{bare noun}; a colour qualifier
  invites the detector to match the colour and fail by returning a large region rather than nothing.
  Sidecar cues are currently written once, by hand, and never swept --- two or three candidates per
  clip, scored on coverage, would cost minutes.
  \item \textbf{Do not replace the tracker --- but the margin is narrower than first measured.}
  LocateAnything-3B was evaluated as a drop-in across all four object-tracked clips. With tuned
  cues it is genuinely close on the turntables ($90$--$99\%$ usable, worst gap $0.03$--$0.25$\,s,
  mean $\omega$ within $0.13\%$), and our initial ``not viable'' reading was substantially an
  artifact of our own cue wording. It still loses on \texttt{roundabout-4046} ($57\%$ usable against
  a continuous track) and it has no temporal propagation to recover from a bad frame, so the
  incumbent stays --- but on this evidence the choice is a trade-off, not a rout.
  \item \textbf{Remove camera drift inside the rectification.} The delivered version uses one
  static axle; tracking it per frame and smoothing over $\sim$2\,s took the prototype's radial
  residual from $3.39\%$ to $1.68\%$ on the same clip. It is the largest remaining
  \emph{measurable} gain and the method already detects the axle in every frame.
  \item \textbf{Make ``was this measured?'' a field, not a convention.} The golden set's
  silver tier --- clips whose orbit is synthesized from a rate rather than tracked
  (\S\ref{sec:fans}) --- is recorded in prose and in the sidecar's \texttt{tracking\_mode}, but
  \emph{not} in \texttt{pipeline\_inputs.json}, so neither the statistics nor the material
  generator can tell a measured trajectory from a generated one. The consequence is live: a
  generated perfect circle is presented to learners as an observation. This is the same class of
  defect as \S\ref{sec:coordbug} --- an agent-asserted fact the pipeline cannot verify --- and
  wants the same treatment.
  \item \textbf{Fix the two \texttt{color\_track.py} bugs} (saturation scoring for dark targets;
  physically-derived \texttt{max\_step}) independently of 4048's fate.
  \item \textbf{Replace coverage with correctness} in the quality gate: box-area stability plus an
  \textbf{angular} step bound derived from $\omega r/\mathrm{fps}$. A fixed pixel threshold both
  misses real failures and manufactures false ones --- it wrongly condemned \texttt{4029}, a clip
  that turned out to be the cleanest in the corpus. The box-area half of this now has a measured
  threshold: rejecting boxes outside $0.45$--$1.8\times$ the clip median area separated good frames
  from failures \emph{exactly} in the ablation (36 of 36 on \texttt{turntable-3}, with every frame
  disagreeing by more than $50$\,px falling on the reject side and none outside it), and coverage
  alone would have passed all of them.
  \item \textbf{Make the visual check mandatory} before any geometry statistic is computed. It
  falsified two confident claims in this investigation and two bugs in its own tooling.
\end{enumerate}
