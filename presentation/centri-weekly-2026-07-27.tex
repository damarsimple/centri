\begin{frame}{Where this comes from}
\scriptsize
\textbf{Centri} turns an ordinary phone video of something spinning into physics worksheets at
\textbf{three levels} --- basic, intermediate, advanced --- all written from the \emph{same}
measured numbers, so they differ in demand without disagreeing on fact. Two things have been asked
of that material: that each level be readable by the reader it is aimed at, and that a reader
\emph{work through} it rather than read it.
\vspace{4pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.4cm}p{7.7cm}c@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cPed!12}
\textbf{What was wrong with it} & \textbf{What the generator does now} & \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\textbf{\color{cPed}Read at the right level} {\color{cGrey}\,--- from a
review by someone who teaches physics in a classroom, the author of P-MAGIC}} \\
the advanced level uses words a high-school student has not met &
the same ideas in plain language; the formal version goes to the teacher's copy & \ok \\
it reaches for the equation before the reader has understood the picture &
the graph is read in words first, then the formula as the compact way to say it & \ok \\
each level is one jump, not a few graded steps &
three graded steps inside every level, each ending in a checkpoint & \ok \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\textbf{\color{cMeas}Do something, rather than read}} \\
nothing is ever asked of the reader first &
predict before the measurement is shown; a check to clear at each step & \ok \\
it never confronts what the reader probably already believes &
the documented traps, posed as questions with the answers held back & \ok \\
one clip binds the idea to one object &
a transfer prompt that moves the same physics somewhere else & \ok \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\takeaway{The three-level architecture is unchanged. What changed is the reading level, the size of
the steps, and whether the reader does anything --- plus one outright error, which is the next
slide.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The direction-sign defect}
\scriptsize
A turn rate carries a sign that records only \textbf{which way round} something turns. Two of the
seven clips turn the way that is recorded as negative, and for those two the software read ``is it
speeding up?'' off the \emph{slope of the signed value} --- and a negative number climbing towards
zero slopes upward.

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.575\textwidth]{fan-sign-4028.png}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{10.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cAccent!10}
& \textbf{what the beginner's worksheet for this ceiling fan says} \\
\midrule
{\color{cAccent}\textbf{before}}~ & ``The fan blade whirls faster and faster as time passes.'' \\
{\color{cApp}\textbf{now}}~ & ``\ldots bends flatter over time, telling us the blade spins fast at
first and then gradually slows down.'' \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\takeaway{Two of seven clips were affected; the other five are unchanged to the digit. The trend is
now taken from the green line above --- the speed --- and the sign is explained wherever one is printed.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Lowering the reading level}
\scriptsize
Three phrases were named as beyond a high-school reader. Each was in the student worksheet because
\textbf{the specification we wrote asked for it} --- so each was a one-line change, not a limit of
the language model.
\vspace{3pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.3cm}p{4.4cm}p{4.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cPed!12}
\textbf{what the student used to read} & \textbf{what it is really saying} & \textbf{where it goes now} \\
\midrule
``which quantities are independent of the \textbf{calibration}'' &
angle measurements do not depend on how we sized the scene; values in metres do &
said to the student in exactly those plain words \\
``the angular quantities are \textbf{scale-free}'' &
the same idea, named a second time &
merged into the sentence above \\
``formally $\langle\omega^2\rangle \ge \langle\omega\rangle^2$, a \textbf{Jensen inequality}'' &
squaring an average is not the same as averaging the squares &
\textbf{teacher's copy only} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{5pt}

Two things make this stick. A separate \textbf{teacher's copy} of every worksheet already existed
and already carried the answer key, so the formal version is re-routed rather than lost. And all
three phrases are now on a list the \textbf{automatic checker refuses}, so the next run cannot
quietly put them back.
\takeaway{Keep the idea, drop the vocabulary, give the formal version to the teacher --- and make
the rule enforceable so it survives the next regeneration.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reading the graph first}
\scriptsize
The intermediate level used to introduce the equation and then show the graph beside it. The order
is now reversed, so the formula arrives as shorthand for something the reader has already seen.
\vspace{4pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cInter!12}
& \textbf{order of the intermediate worksheet} \\
\midrule
{\color{cAccent}\textbf{before}}~ & the scene $\rightarrow$ the measured quantities $\rightarrow$
\textbf{how they are related} $\rightarrow$ what the video shows over time \\
{\color{cApp}\textbf{now}}~ & the scene $\rightarrow$ the measured quantities $\rightarrow$
\textbf{what the video shows over time} $\rightarrow$ how they are related \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{6pt}

Real output from the reworked generator, on the ceiling-fan clip. The graph section carries no
formula at all, and the next section opens by pointing back at it:
\vspace{3pt}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=cInter!5,colframe=cInter,boxsep=2pt,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=3pt,bottom=3pt]
\scriptsize
\textbf{What the video shows over time.} ``The graph of the turn rate over time tells the story of
the coasting spin. \ldots\ Where the graph is high, the turn rate is large and the blade is whipping
around quickly; where the graph drops, the turn rate shrinks and the blade sweeps through smaller
angles each second.''
\vspace{2pt}

\textbf{How the variables are related.} ``\emph{The falling turn rate on the graph} directly controls
the inward pull through the relation $a_{\mathrm{c}} = \omega^2 r$.''
\end{tcolorbox}
\takeaway{Interpret before you calculate --- the equation is introduced as a description of a
picture the reader has already read, not as the thing to be understood first.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three graded steps}
\scriptsize
Every level is now a staircase of three steps, printed at the top of the worksheet so a reader can
see which rung they are on, with a bridge carrying them onto the first step of the next level.
\vspace{4pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cp{2.9cm}p{4.3cm}p{4.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cPed!12}
& \textbf{\color{cBasic}basic} & \textbf{\color{cInter}intermediate} & \textbf{\color{cAdv}advanced} \\
\midrule
\textbf{1} & what the words mean &
read the graph in words, no formula at all &
compare two moments \\
\textbf{2} & what this clip's numbers are &
meet the equation, and put this clip's numbers in &
compare the phases of the motion \\
\textbf{3} & which idea joins them &
how long it took to slow from this rate to that one &
which claims does this video actually support? \\
\midrule
\textbf{bridge} & \multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{step 3 of each level names the step it hands over to ---
one staircase, not three separate documents} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{6pt}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{cApp}Each step ends in a checkpoint} --- one question to clear before the next step
starts, printed where that step actually ends rather than listed at the top. A named rung a reader
never has to stand on is a signpost, not a staircase.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{cMeas}And the level is chosen, not assigned.} Each edition opens by stating what it
assumes and naming the level above and below, so a reader who is in the wrong one can move without
being tested first.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\takeaway{The thinking level separates the three levels; the steps inside each one, and the check
that closes each step, make it reachable from the level below.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{From reading to doing}
\scriptsize
The material was \textbf{exposition}: a well-graded passage to read. Reading is among the weakest
routes into physics, and a worksheet built from a video can do better --- a textbook cannot stop
before the reveal. Four additions, none written by the language model, every answer held back for
the teacher's copy.
\vspace{3pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.28}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.5cm}p{4.5cm}p{4.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cMeas!12}
\textbf{What the reader does} & \textbf{Where it sits} & \textbf{Why there} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Predicts} & before any measurement is shown &
a commitment made first is what makes the reveal informative \\
\textbf{Clears a checkpoint} & at the end of each of the three steps &
a rung named at the top is one a reader never has to stand on \\
\textbf{Meets the traps} & after the honesty box &
the wrong answer most readers hold has to be said out loud to be given up \\
\textbf{Moves it elsewhere} & last, before the closing questions &
if the idea only works on the object just watched, it has not been understood \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{5pt}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{cAdv}The advanced examples now fade.} The first is worked in full as a model; the
rest show the setup and stop, leaving the last step to the reader. A fully worked example is what a
beginner needs and what stops helping once the pattern is there.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{cAccent}An honest limit.} The material never names a cause --- no forces --- so the
misconceptions that live there (a spinning object ``flung outward'') stay out of reach. The traps
it does pose are the ones a motion-only treatment can actually correct.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\takeaway{Every one of these is computed, not written, so it is correct on every clip without a
language model being trusted to invent it.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Measuring the ladder}
\scriptsize
Whether the three levels really do get harder is \emph{measured} from the finished prose, not
asserted. Averages over all seven clips. One measure always held; the other was failing, and
measuring \emph{why} turned it into a one-line fix.
\vspace{3pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.28}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.2cm}ccc l@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cPed!12}
& \textbf{\color{cBasic}basic} & \textbf{\color{cInter}intermediate} & \textbf{\color{cAdv}advanced}
& \textbf{verdict} \\
\midrule
\textbf{ideas held at once} {\color{cGrey}\,--- quantities plus the relations linking them}
& 5 & 19 & 44 & \ok\ rises, 7/7 clips \\
\textbf{words per sentence} {\color{cGrey}\,--- was 15.8 / 10.1 / 11.5} & 8.2 & 7.2 & 12.0 &
\ok\ advanced longest, not basic \\
\textbf{reading grade} {\color{cGrey}\,--- was 7.2 / 5.2 / 6.7} & 4.3 & 3.7 & 6.6 &
\ok\ advanced hardest, not basic \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{4pt}

The \textbf{basic} worksheet used to score the \emph{hardest} reading grade of the three --- the
level aimed at the least fluent reader. Splitting that score into its two causes showed it was not
the vocabulary: syllables per word barely move between levels (1.41 / 1.43 / 1.51). It was
\textbf{sentence length}: basic explained in long sentences where the other levels state in short
ones. Asking that level for short sentences took it from 15.8 words to 8.2.
\vspace{2pt}

{\scriptsize\color{cGrey}Precisely: the three are still not strictly ordered by reading grade ---
intermediate reads easiest of all. What changed is that the level for the weakest reader is no
longer the hardest to read.}
\takeaway{Plain words and plain sentences are not the same thing. Measuring which of the two was
responsible turned something that looked like a design trade-off into a defect with a fix.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Showing the correction}
\scriptsize
Where a measurement is adjusted, the worksheet now draws \textbf{both} lines --- what came off the
video, and what it was corrected to. Real output from the shipped run on the playground-wheel clip:
\vspace{1pt}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.495\textwidth]{wheel-ac-shipped.png}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
The wheel is filmed at a slant, so the handle appears to race and stall once per turn. That is the
\textbf{camera position}, not the wheel --- and it lands hardest in the inward pull, which follows
the turn rate \emph{squared}.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
The swing narrows from \textbf{7.8--31.8} to \textbf{9.8--23.0}\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} while the
average barely moves (16.2 to 16.1) --- so a reader can \emph{see} that the correction redistributed
the motion within each turn rather than rescaling it.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\takeaway{The figure now carries the argument the honesty note used to make in prose, and every
adjustment names itself in the legend.}
\end{frame}

The basic level used to get a stripped-down still marking only the radius, while the two harder

symbols --- which is the rule that separates this level, and is untouched by the change.

\begin{frame}{Checking every worksheet}
\scriptsize
Every worksheet is checked before it ships: arithmetic that closes on its own printed numbers, no
number that is not in the measurement, vocabulary suited to the level, and a description of the
motion that matches the graph beside it. All seven clips were then regenerated from scratch. After
the fixes below, \textbf{21 of the 21 worksheets pass every check}.
\vspace{2pt}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{6.3cm}p{6.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{cMeas!12}
\textbf{What the regeneration flagged} & \textbf{What it turned out to be} \\
\midrule
the elapsed time between two instants --- which is the answer to the new third step & \\
``a brief steady spin'', on a clip whose graph draws a steady band and prints that word on it & \\
``(average turn rate) squared $\times$ radius'' in a worked example & \\
``this clip covers only 1.47 revolutions'' --- a sentence the pipeline instructs the writer to include &
\multirow{-4}{6.3cm}{\raggedright \textbf{\color{cAccent}Four times the checker was wrong}, each
time refusing wording or a number the pipeline had itself handed the writer. All four are fixed and
pinned by a test.} \\
\midrule
the word \emph{momentum} in a kinematics passage; an ``='' sign in the symbol-free level &
\textbf{\color{cApp}Two real errors}, on two clips --- and the model repeated the first on both
retries, the known limit of the 35-billion-parameter model we run locally: it drafts well, corrects
itself poorly. Both cleared on the regenerated run. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\takeaway{Four of the six flags were the checker refusing writing the pipeline itself asked for ---
a fault that costs quality silently, so it matters more than the pass rate.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What is still unproven}
\scriptsize
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=cAccent!6,colframe=cAccent,boxsep=1pt,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=2pt,bottom=2pt]
\scriptsize Everything measured here is a property of the \textbf{text}: how many ideas it makes the
reader hold, how long its sentences run, whether its numbers trace to the video. Nothing yet measures
whether anyone \emph{learns} from it. P-MAGIC has ten teachers and a rating of 8.2--8.6/10; this
material has one expert reader --- whose review, to its credit, found a real correctness bug.
\end{tcolorbox}
